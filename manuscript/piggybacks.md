## 0. Setting

### 0.1 Structures and model sets

A **QmDAG** ([`quantum_mDAG.QmDAG`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L66)) is a causal structure with visible nodes $V=\lbrace 0,\dots,n-1\rbrace$, a directed structure on $V$, and two simplicial complexes of **latent facets**: classical facets $\mathcal C$ and quantum facets $\mathcal Q$. A facet $F\subseteq V$ stands for one exogenous latent, a classical random variable or a quantum state, whose children are exactly the nodes of $F$. Every visible node additionally has implicit private randomness. A classical facet contained in a quantum facet is redundant and is dropped by the constructor.

For a structure $G$ write $\mathcal C(G)$ for the set of distributions over $V$ realisable with all latents classical (a Bayesian network with latent variables), and $\mathcal Q(G)$ for the set realisable when the quantum facets carry quantum states on which their children perform measurements, classical facets and visible edges remaining classical channels. Always $\mathcal C(G)\subseteq\mathcal Q(G)$. The structure $G$ has a **QC gap** when $\mathcal Q(G)\not\subseteq\mathcal C(G)$.

The **effective DAG** of a QmDAG ([`QmDAG.effective_DAG_data`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L450)) is the ordinary DAG on the visible nodes, one node per facet and one private-noise node per visible node; [`QmDAG.lp_structure`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L487) is the same without the noise nodes. A "parent" of a visible node below is a parent in the effective DAG: a visible parent or a facet containing the node.

### 0.2 Piggybacks

A **piggyback** is a map $G\mapsto G'$ together with a proof of

```math
\mathcal Q(G')\not\subseteq\mathcal C(G')\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathcal Q(G)\not\subseteq\mathcal C(G).
```

Every proof in this document has the same two halves.

* **Quantum lift.** Given $P'\in\mathcal Q(G')\setminus\mathcal C(G')$, construct $P\in\mathcal Q(G)$ from which $P'$ is recovered by a fixed operation $\pi$ (a marginal, a conditional, a coarse-graining, or the identity): $\pi(P)=P'$.
* **Classical pull-back.** Show that every classical model of $G$ for a distribution of the kind the lift produces yields a classical model of $G'$ for $\pi(P)$.

Then $P\in\mathcal C(G)$ would give $P'=\pi(P)\in\mathcal C(G')$, a contradiction, so $P\in\mathcal Q(G)\setminus\mathcal C(G)$.

All piggybacks here are **caveat-free**: the hypothesis is a QC gap of $G'$ as a structure, with no side condition on the gap-witnessing distribution. This is what lets them be composed in any order and searched as a closure (Section [9](#9-search-certificates-and-the-four-node-census-qc_gap_searchpy-special-applicationsproving_qc_gapspy)). Structures are identified up to relabelling by [`unique_unlabelled_id`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L153), and every count in this document is a count of distinct structures up to relabelling.

### 0.3 Two general facts

* **(F1) Edge monotonicity.** If $G'$ is obtained from $G$ only by deleting visible edges and removing nodes from facets, then $\mathcal Q(G')\subseteq\mathcal Q(G)$ and $\mathcal C(G')\subseteq\mathcal C(G)$: run the same strategy and ignore the extra inputs. For an edge-deleting piggyback the lift is therefore immediate and the whole content is the pull-back.
* **(F2) d-separation.** Among the *visible* variables, every d-separation of $G$ implies the corresponding conditional independence, in classical and in quantum models alike: observable d-separation is theory-independent (Henson, Lal and Pusey). Conversely no other conditional independence among visible variables holds in every classical model, hence none holds in every quantum model either, since $\mathcal C(G)\subseteq\mathcal Q(G)$. Conditional independences that *involve latents* are statements about a joint distribution of visible and latent variables. Such a joint exists for a classical model, where every node of the effective DAG is a random variable, but not in general for a quantum one: a quantum facet is a state, not a variable, and only coexisting sets of nodes have a joint distribution (Chaves, Majenz and Gross). The pull-backs below reason about classical models only, so they may use latent-involving d-separations and the entropies of the effective DAG freely.

### 0.4 The degradation piggyback (quantum source to classical source)

**Map.** $G'$ is $G$ with some quantum facets made classical. Equivalently, $G$ is $G'$ with some classical facets upgraded to quantum, or with quantum facets added inside existing classical facets (a quantum facet contained in a classical one is a special case of upgrading part of that classical facet).

**Pull-back.** $\mathcal C(G)=\mathcal C(G')$: in a classical model every facet, quantum or classical, is one latent random variable with the facet as its children, and a latent whose facet lies inside another facet's is redundant, so the two structures have the same classical models. **Lift.** $\mathcal Q(G')\subseteq\mathcal Q(G)$: a classical source is a quantum state that happens to be diagonal. Hence $\mathcal Q(G')\not\subseteq\mathcal C(G')$ implies $\mathcal Q(G)\not\subseteq\mathcal C(G)$. $\square$

This is the facet counterpart of (F1) and the only piggyback that changes no edge and no node. It says that a QC gap is **monotone under upgrading sources**: the structures worth recording are the *weakest* ones, with as many facets classical as the gap allows, and every structure above them in the upgrade order is a gap for free. Example: G(3) of [6.5](#65-examples), 0→1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, C{0,1,3}, C{0,1,2}, has a gap (one teleportation marginalization to [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41)), hence so do

* 0→1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, Q{1,2}, C{0,1,3}, C{0,1,2} (a quantum facet added inside C{0,1,2});
* 0→1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, Q{1,2}, Q{0,3}, C{0,1,3}, C{0,1,2}, and likewise with Q{1,3}, or with both;
* 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{1,2}, Q{0,1,3}, C{0,1,2} (C{0,1,3} upgraded; it absorbs Q{0,1});
* 0→1→2; C{0,1,3}, Q{0,1,2} (C{0,1,2} upgraded; it absorbs Q{0,1} and Q{0,2});
* and so on up to 0→1→2; Q{0,1,3}, Q{0,1,2}, every source quantum.

The search applies it as a **lookup**, not as an expansion ([`degradation`](../qc_gap_search.py#L55), [`ClosureExplorer.lookup_tricks`](../qc_gap_search.py#L174)): every structure the search meets, input or intermediate, has its degradations registered as transitions whose targets are never expanded, so a structure is known as soon as any of its degradations is, at the cost of one id computation per degradation and no search. Expanding the degradations would multiply the structures to explore by up to $2^k$ and could prove nothing new, since a degradation is harder to prove than the structure it comes from. The seed list is kept in **weakest form only** ([`known_QC_gaps.SEEDS`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L108): a named gap is dropped when another named gap is a degradation of it, and one representative per relabelling class is kept), so [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41) stands for its all-quantum version 1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{1,2}, [`QG_Triangle`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L42) for all three triangle colourings, [`QG_Bell_C_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L78) for the Bell variants with one or both setting facets quantum, and so on; certificates end with the degradation step that reaches the weakest form.

### 0.5 The census, the seeds and the names of the tricks

The **census** (Section [9](#9-search-certificates-and-the-four-node-census-qc_gap_searchpy-special-applicationsproving_qc_gapspy)) is the set of four-node mDAGs whose directed edges respect the order $0\lt1\lt2\lt3$ and that are not provably algebraic, with every latent facet quantum; these are the **inputs**. The **seeds** ([`known_QC_gaps.py`](../known_QC_gaps.py)) are structures with an established QC gap: the instrumental, triangle and Evans variants with three visible nodes, and the Bell variants with four. The census runs in two phases (Section [9](#9-search-certificates-and-the-four-node-census-qc_gap_searchpy-special-applicationsproving_qc_gapspy)): a cheap phase in which only the elementary reductions act and only the three-node seeds are known, so that the Bell variants are inputs, and an expensive phase in which the weakest Bell variants are seeds and the Fritz trick is applied, in four stages, to whatever the cheap phase left. An input is **proven** when a chain of piggybacks leads from it to a seed or to an input already proven; a proven structure is a **known gap**. "Provable only through $T$" means that the input is no longer proven when every transition of trick $T$ is removed from the record and everything else is kept (Section [9](#9-search-certificates-and-the-four-node-census-qc_gap_searchpy-special-applicationsproving_qc_gapspy)).

The **tricks** are the piggybacks as the search applies them: `PD`, [`node_stitching`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L306), `conditioning`, `naive_marginalization` and `teleportation_marginalization` (the **elementary reductions**, which lower the number of visible nodes), the lookup [`degradation`](../qc_gap_search.py#L55) ([0.4](#04-the-degradation-piggyback-quantum-source-to-classical-source)), and the single trick `Fritz` (Sections [6](#6-the-fritz-piggyback-fritz) and [7](#7-the-entropic-certificate-for-the-fritz-piggyback-qmdag_entropic_certificate)), whose transitions record the predicted-node mode, the predictor mode and the certificate, `dsep` or `entropic`. The search runs the Fritz trick in **stages** named after these parameters (Section [9](#9-search-certificates-and-the-four-node-census-qc_gap_searchpy-special-applicationsproving_qc_gapspy)). Two nodes are **latent siblings** when they share a facet, **quantum siblings** when they share a quantum facet.

## 2. Point distribution (`PD`)

**Map.** Remove one visible node $v$ together with all its edges and its membership in facets; write $G-v$ for the result, so $G'=G-v$ ([`QmDAG.fix_to_point_distribution_QmDAG`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L177); trick `PD`, [`QmDAG.pd_steps`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L579)).

**Lift.** Given $P'\in\mathcal Q(G')$, let $v$ output a constant and every other node run its $G'$ strategy; $\pi$ is the marginal over $v$. $P\in\mathcal Q(G)$ because the children of $v$ may ignore a constant input.

**Pull-back.** In a classical model of $G$ for a distribution with $v$ constant, substitute the constant into the kernels of $v$'s children; the result is a classical model of $G-v$ for the marginal. $\square$

**Example** (provable only through PD; 221 such inputs). Input: 0→2, 0→3; Q{0,1,2}, Q{0,1,3}, Q{2,3}. Fixing 0 to a point distribution deletes its two edges and removes it from the two triple facets, leaving Q{1,2}, Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}: the all-quantum triangle, which degrades to [`QG_Triangle`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L42). Most PD proofs look like this: a seed with one more node attached to it.

## 3. Marginalization, naive and with teleportation (`naive_marginalization`, `teleportation_marginalization`)

**Map** ([`QmDAG.marginalize(node, apply_teleportation)`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L342)). Remove $v$; every visible parent of $v$ becomes a parent of every visible child of $v$; every facet containing $v$ loses $v$ (a quantum facet stays quantum on $F\setminus v$) and in addition a *classical* facet over $(F\setminus v)$ and all visible children of $v$ is added; a new classical facet over the visible children of $v$ is added as well (the private randomness of $v$). With teleportation, additionally every quantum facet $F\ni v$ is enlarged to a quantum facet on $(F\setminus v)\cup T$, where $T$ is the set of visible children of $v$ that share some quantum facet with $v$. $\pi$ is the marginal over $v$.

**Pull-back.** Without teleportation $G'$ is the latent projection of $G$ over $v$ (Evans), and the marginal of a classical model of $G$ is a classical model of the projection: the kernel of $v$ is absorbed into its children, its latent parents are relayed to its children, and its private randomness becomes a common cause of its children. With teleportation $G'$ has more facets than the projection, and $\mathcal C$ only grows with facets. $\square$

**Lift.** Given $P'\in\mathcal Q(G')$: $v$ receives its visible parents and forwards them to its children (the new visible edges), forwards the classical values its facets carry (a quantum facet may carry the classical string that the new classical facet over $(F\setminus v)$ and the children uses in $G'$) and broadcasts a private random string (the new classical facet over the children). For a quantum facet $F\ni v$ that in $G'$ extends to the children in $T$: $v$ holds a composite share of the $G'$-state, one subsystem per child in $T$; $v$ and each such child $c$ share some quantum facet, which may carry an extra maximally entangled pair, and $v$ teleports the subsystem destined for $c$ through that pair and the visible edge $v\to c$. The children then hold exactly the resources of $G'$. $\square$

*Order dependence.* Teleportation is not symmetric, so marginalizing several nodes in different orders can give different structures; wherever the code removes several nodes it enumerates every order ([`QmDAG.fritz_realise`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L769), through [`QmDAG._marginalize_nodes`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L756)).

**Example** (provable only through marginalization; one of the 24 such inputs of the cheap phase, [1.1](#11-phase-1-results-the-elementary-piggybacks-from-the-three-node-seeds)). Input: 0→1→2→3; Q{1,3}. Node 2 relays the outcome of 1 to 3 and has no latent of its own. Marginalizing it relays the edge, 0→1→3; Q{1,3}, which is [`QG_Instrumental_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L39). Nothing else works: PD on 2 leaves 3 without an input; conditioning on 2 is inadmissible because the grandparent 0 is not a parent of 2, and after PD on 0 it is still inadmissible by condition 2 of Section [4](#4-conditioning-conditioning), since the facet Q{1,3} contains the parent 1 but not 2. Both marginalizations give the same output here, since 2 has no quantum facet. The same pattern, a chain through a latent-free relay node, accounts for all three inputs; another is 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}, where marginalizing 1 relays the setting 0 to 2 and gives 0→2; Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}, which degrades to [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41).

## 4. Conditioning (`conditioning`)

**Map** ([`QmDAG.condition(node)`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L273)). Remove $X$; add a classical facet over $\mathrm{Pa}(X)\cap V$ together with the latent siblings of $X$ (the visible nodes sharing some facet with $X$); add a quantum facet over the nodes sharing a quantum facet with $X$. $\pi(P)=P(\cdot\mid X=x_0)$ for a suitable value $x_0$.

**Admissibility** ([`QmDAG.conditioning_is_justified`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L249)). Writing $B=\mathrm{Pa}(X)\cap V$ for the visible parents and $S$ for the latent siblings of $X$:

1. every visible grandparent of $X$ is a visible parent of $X$, so $B$ is ancestrally closed among visible nodes;
2. every facet containing a node of $B$ either contains $X$ or is contained in $B$;
3. every visible child of a parent $p\in B\setminus S$ (a parent sharing no facet with $X$) lies in $B\cup S\cup\lbrace X\rbrace$.

Conditions 1 and 2 say that no grandparent of $X$, visible or latent, may fail to be a parent of $X$, except for latents whose children all lie in $B$, which only redistribute the parent block. Condition 3 comes from the lift below: a parent that shares no facet with $X$ can only learn the post-selected common cause by guessing it and announcing the guess in its output, and that fine-grained output must not be readable by nodes outside the new facet. Condition 2 is needed for the pull-back as stated; whether the implication itself can fail without it is not settled, but the pull-back lemma does fail. Take $X$ with parents $p_1,p_2$, each $p_i$ sharing a classical facet with an outside node $o_i$, and the classical model $p_i=\lambda_i\oplus\varepsilon_i$ with biased noise, $o_i=\lambda_i$, $X=[p_1=p_2]$. Conditioning on $X=1$ makes $o_1$ and $o_2$ dependent ($I(o_1{:}o_2)\approx0.046$ bits), while $G'$ d-separates them; so $\pi(\mathcal C(G))\not\subseteq\mathcal C(G')$, which is what the pull-back needs ([`tests/test_conditioning.py`](../tests/test_conditioning.py)).

**Pull-back.** Let $\Lambda_X$ be the latents of $X$ and $\Lambda_B$ the latents whose children all lie in $B$. By conditions 1 and 2 every input of a node in $B$ lies in $B$, in $\Lambda_X$ or in $\Lambda_B$; the latents of $\Lambda_B$ influence nothing outside $B$; and every other latent is independent of $(B,X,\Lambda_X,\Lambda_B)$. In a classical model of $G$ the conditional distribution therefore factorises as

```math
P(\text{all}\mid x_0)\ =\ P(\Lambda_X,\Lambda_B\mid x_0)\cdot P(B\mid \Lambda_X,\Lambda_B,x_0)\cdot\Big[\prod_{\text{other nodes}}\text{kernels}\Big]\cdot P(\text{other latents}),
```

where the block distribution $P(B\mid\Lambda_X,\Lambda_B,x_0)$ is proportional to $\prod_{p\in B}P(p\mid\mathrm{pa}(p)\cap B,\Lambda_X,\Lambda_B)\cdot P(x_0\mid B,\Lambda_X)$: the product of the block's kernels, reweighted by the probability that $X$ takes the value $x_0$. Define the new latent $\mu=(\Lambda_X,\Lambda_B,R)$, where $(\Lambda_X,\Lambda_B)$ is drawn from the posterior $P(\Lambda_X,\Lambda_B\mid x_0)$ and $R$ is fresh uniform randomness. The latent siblings of $X$ read $\Lambda_X$ through $\mu$; this is legitimate because every facet containing $X$ lies within $S\cup\lbrace X\rbrace$, so nobody else reads $\Lambda_X$ (the facets $F\setminus X$ that $G'$ retains are set trivial). Visible children of $X$, if any, lose the edge from $X$ and read the constant $x_0$ instead. The block $B$ is sampled jointly, as deterministic functions of $\mu$ alone, from the reweighted block distribution: all inputs of the block are available in $\mu$, so the shared seed $R$ lets each $p$ compute its coordinate of one joint sample. Every other node keeps its kernel. The result is a classical model of $G'$ for $P(\cdot\mid x_0)$. Without condition 2 some $p$ has a latent shared with outsiders, the reweighted block depends on inputs the other block members cannot see, and the counterexample above is exactly this failure. $\square$

**Lift.** Given $P'\in\mathcal Q(G')$ with the new classical facet $\mu$ and the new quantum state $\rho$ over the quantum siblings $S_Q$ of $X$. In $G$: every facet $F\ni X$ carries an independent uniform copy $\mu_F$ of the string $\mu$; every quantum facet $F\ni X$ additionally carries one maximally entangled pair between $X$ and each quantum sibling in $F$. Latent siblings, and parents in $B\cap S$, read $\mu_F$ from a facet they share with $X$. $X$ prepares $\rho$ locally and teleports each subsystem to its sibling through the corresponding pair; post-selected teleportation, keeping only the identity outcome of each Bell measurement, leaves $S_Q$ holding $\rho$ exactly. A parent $p\in B\setminus S$ has no facet with $X$: it draws a private guess $\hat\mu_p$, runs its $G'$ strategy with $\mu:=\hat\mu_p$, and outputs $(p,\hat\mu_p)$ so that $X$ can check the guess. $X$ sets $X=x_0$ iff all $\mu_F$ and all $\hat\mu_p$ coincide and every Bell outcome is the identity. Conditioned on $X=x_0$ the strings are a single uniform $\mu$ shared by $B$ and the siblings, $S_Q$ shares $\rho$, and every node has run its $G'$ strategy.

The guessing parents' outputs are fine-grainings $(p,\hat\mu_p)$ of their $G'$ outputs, and this is where condition 3 is needed. Coarse-graining a node's output is not in general an operation under which $\mathcal C(G')$ is closed: a classical model for the fine-grained distribution may let the children of $p$ read $\hat\mu_p$ through the visible edge, and no model for the coarsened distribution need exist (Section [6](#6-the-fritz-piggyback-fritz).2 gives a structure where exactly this fails). Under condition 3 the children of a guessing parent are $X$, other parents or latent siblings, all of which read $\mu$ from the new facet of $G'$, so in the classical model of $G'$ that the pull-back constructs, where $\hat\mu_p=\mu$ is a function of the new facet, every use of the second component of $p$'s output can be rerouted through the facet, and the coarsened distribution $P'$ is in $\mathcal C(G')$ whenever the fine-grained one is. $\square$

**Example** (provable only through conditioning; 23 such inputs). Input: 2→3; Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}, Q{1,2}. Node 0 has no parents, so all three conditions hold vacuously. Conditioning on 0 removes it and adds a quantum facet over its quantum siblings 2 and 3 (the classical facet over the same pair is absorbed), giving 2→3; Q{1,2}, Q{2,3}, the all-quantum version of [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41). Operationally the lift is entanglement swapping: 0 performs a Bell measurement on its two shares and the post-selected outcome leaves 2 and 3 entangled. Among the reductions only conditioning and teleportation marginalization create a facet between nodes that did not share one, and teleportation needs a visible edge from 0 to a quantum sibling, which is absent here.

## 5. Node stitching (`node_stitching`), formerly interruption

**Map** ([`QmDAG.node_stitching(node_with_no_children=y, node_with_no_parents=x)`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L306)). $x$ is exogenous (no visible parents, no non-singleton facet: [`QmDAG.exogenous_visible_nodes`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L114)) and $y$ is not a descendant of $x$, so that $G'$ is acyclic. Remove $x$; its visible children become children of $y$. The search additionally restricts $y$ to childless nodes; the proof does not need this. $\pi$ is the renormalised diagonal $x=y$ of $P$:

```math
\pi(P)(y,\text{rest})=\frac{P(x{=}y,\;y,\;\text{rest})}{P_x(y)},
```

where $P_x$ is the marginal of $x$ (uniform for every lifted distribution, so the denominator is then a constant).

**Lift.** Given $P'\in\mathcal Q(G')$, in $G$ let $x$ be uniform, let the former children of $x$ treat the value of $x$ as they treated $y$ in $G'$, and let every other node run its $G'$ strategy. Then $P(x,y,\text{rest})=P_x(x)\,P'^{\,do(y:=x)}(y,\text{rest})$, where the right factor is the distribution of the $G'$ strategy with the input that the former children of $x$ now take from $y$ fixed to $x$ (an edge intervention). Fixing that input to the value $y$ actually takes changes nothing, so $P'^{\,do(y:=y)}=P'$ and $\pi(P)=P'$.

**Pull-back.** Let $M$ be a classical model of $G$ for a distribution with $x$ uniform; $x$ is independent of all latents of $M$. Define a model of $G'$ in which every node keeps its kernel, except that the former children of $x$ receive $y$ in place of $x$. Since $y\notin\mathrm{desc}(x)$, the value $Y(\lambda)$ does not depend on $x$ and the new model is acyclic; its distribution is $\sum_\lambda p(\lambda)[y=Y(\lambda)]\,[\text{rest}=f(\lambda,x{:=}Y(\lambda))]$, which is the diagonal $x=Y(\lambda)$ of $M$'s distribution divided by $P_x(y)$, that is $\pi(P)$. Other children of $y$ read $y$ in both structures. $\square$

**Naming.** The map *stitches* the exogenous node $x$ onto the sink $y$ by post-selecting $x=y$. Its inverse, which cuts a node into an exogenous source and a sink and distributes the children ([Appendix A](#appendix-a-the-piggybacks-in-reverse)), is the operation that deserves the name interruption, under which this piggyback was previously filed.

**Example.** [`QG_Bell_Edge_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L83) is 0→2, 1→3; Q{2,3}. Stitching with $x=1$ (exogenous) and $y=2$ (childless, not a descendant of 1) removes 1 and makes 3 a child of 2: 0→2→3; Q{2,3}, which is [`QG_Instrumental_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L39). The post-selection $x=y$ identifies Bob's setting with Alice's outcome. Read as a piggyback: a gap in the instrumental scenario implies a gap in the Bell scenario. Likewise 0→2, 1→3; Q{1,3}, Q{2,3} maps with $x=0$, $y=3$ to 1→3→2; Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}, which degrades to [`QG_Instrumental_EdgeC`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L40), and [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80) (0→2; C{1,3}, Q{2,3}) maps to [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41).

## 6. The Fritz piggyback (`Fritz`)

**Notation.** Parents are taken in the effective DAG: $\mathrm{Pa}(s)$ consists of the visible parents of $s$, the facets containing $s$ and the private noise of $s$. The predictor is in general a **set** of visible nodes, written $\mathbf X$; its members are the predictors. $\mathrm{Pa}(\mathbf X)=\bigcup_{x\in\mathbf X}\mathrm{Pa}(x)$. The nodes **seen** by $\mathbf X$ are $\mathrm{Pa}(\mathbf X)\cup\mathbf X$. For a predicted node $s$,

```math
\mathrm{common}(s)=\mathrm{Pa}(s)\cap\big(\mathrm{Pa}(\mathbf X)\cup\mathbf X\big),\qquad
\mathrm{others}(s)=\mathrm{Pa}(s)\setminus\mathrm{common}(s),
```

the latter always containing the private noise of $s$. Structures in examples are written as a list of visible edges and facets, with Q for quantum and C for classical facets, e.g. "0→2, 1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}".

### 6.1 The mechanism

Both Fritz piggybacks are instances of one scheme:

> choose a set of edges of $G$ to delete, giving $G'\subseteq G$, and justify the deletion by a **perfect correlation** that the quantum lift can arrange, between a subvariable of a predictor and the predicted node, and that forces the deleted dependences to be idle in every classical model.

Fix a predictor set $\mathbf X$ and a predicted node $s\notin\mathbf X$ with a **channel** from a predictor to $s$: a facet shared by $s$ and a predictor, or an edge $X\to s$ from a predictor that is a visible parent of $s$ (the second kind is an option of the search, Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks)). The words *predictor* and *predicted*, which are the code's, name the roles; the relation itself is symmetric, following KPC: the lift gives $\mathbf X$ a subvariable $X_S$ with $X_S=s$, a **perfect correlation**, and nothing flows from $\mathbf X$ to $s$, both being functions of shared inputs. The certificates consume only one direction of it, $H(s\mid\mathbf X)=0$, which is called **perfect prediction** below; the two are interconvertible (given $H(s\mid\mathbf X)=0$, $X_S:=g(\mathbf X)$ is a subvariable of the predictors' fine-grained output equal to $s$), so nothing is lost by stating the hypothesis in the weaker form. Since $\mathrm{others}(s)$ always contains the private noise of $s$, the d-separation test below fails for every predictor that is a descendant of $s$ (the LP of Section [7](#7-the-entropic-certificate-for-the-fritz-piggyback-qmdag_entropic_certificate) does not, Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks)). The **candidate** $G_1$ restricts $s$ to $\mathrm{common}(s)$ and leaves everything else in place, predictors included: visible co-parents are kept; facets of $\mathrm{common}(s)$ are kept but become classical *for $s$* (a quantum facet $F$ keeps its quantum part on $F\setminus\lbrace s\rbrace$ and a classical facet over $F$ is added, which is the state $\rho\otimes\sigma$ with $\sigma$ classical). The **output** $G'$ of the piggyback is $G_1$ with the predictors removed, deleted if childless and marginalized otherwise (Section [3](#3-marginalization-naive-and-with-teleportation-naive_marginalization-teleportation_marginalization)), or kept in the split form of Section [6](#6-the-fritz-piggyback-fritz). Apart from the added classical facet $G_1\subseteq G$, so by (F1) the lift from $G_1$ to $G$ is the construction of the correlation, and the entire content is the pull-back: a classical model of $G$ in which the prediction holds, and in which the other observable properties of the lifted distribution hold, is also a classical model of $G_1$. Throughout, "Markov($H$)" for a DAG $H$ means the local Markov property, each node independent of its non-descendants given its parents, which for a DAG is equivalent to the factorisation of the joint into one kernel per node.

The lift lets $s$ be a deterministic function $f$ of $\mathrm{common}(s)$, with its private randomness drawn from a facet shared with a predictor (or computed by a predictor that is a parent of $s$ and sent down the edge), and lets each predictor output the part of $f(\mathrm{common}(s))$ computable from what it sees, so that $\mathbf X$ jointly carries a subvariable $X_S$ equal to $s$: the perfect correlation. This is why $s$ needs a channel from a predictor (noise absorption) and why only $\mathrm{common}(s)$ may be kept: the predictors must be able to compute $s$.

### 6.2 Node splitting and the two predictor modes (`QmDAG.split_node`)

**Map.** Replace $s$ by two nodes $s,s'$ with the same parents and the same children, sharing a classical two-party facet $\lbrace s,s'\rbrace$ (their common private randomness; absorbed when $s$ already lies in a facet). $\pi$ merges $(s,s')$ into one node.

**Lift.** $s$ computes both outputs from its inputs and private randomness. **Pull-back.** A classical kernel for the pair $(s,s')$ splits into two kernels sharing the randomness carried by the new facet. $\square$ When the absorbing facet is quantum the pair's shared randomness is carried by that quantum facet, which is legitimate because the pull-back concerns classical models only.

Node splitting is never run on its own: the split structure has one more visible node and no new independences, so it is only a stepping stone. It enters the Fritz piggybacks in two different roles, and the difference matters.

* **Splitting a predictor.** The Fritz lift makes each predictor $x\in\mathbf X$ output, alongside its own value, the part of $f(\mathrm{common}(s))$ it can compute: the predictor's output is *fine-grained*, and the extra component is the subvariable $X_S$ perfectly correlated with $s$. The pull-back ends with a classical model of $G'$ in which $x$ still has the fine-grained output, and the proof must get rid of the extra component. If $x$ is dropped from $G'$, deleted when childless or marginalized otherwise, the component is integrated out and nothing more is needed (`dropped` predictor mode). If $x$ is to stay in $G'$, the component has to be coarse-grained away, and **coarse-graining the output of a node with children is unsound**: a classical model of $G'$ for the fine-grained distribution may let the children of $x$ read the correlated subvariable $X_S$ through the visible edge, and no model for the coarsened distribution need exist. 

  Example: 0→1; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, Q{1,2} is saturated (latent-free equivalent, so it has no gap), yet "0 predicts 2, 0 kept untouched" would output 0→1; C{0,2}, Q{0,1}, which is [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41): node 1 learns the instrument 2 from 0's fine-grained output, which is exactly what the Bonet inequality forbids the binary treatment to transmit. 

  The sound way to keep a predictor is node splitting with a *full* copy: $x$ is split into $x$ and $x'$ with the same parents and the same children, $x'$ is the predictor, and $x'$ is dropped as above. For a childless $x$ this coincides with keeping $x$ untouched (the childless copy is deleted). For a childful $x$ the copy is marginalized, which relays to $x$'s children everything the correlated subvariable could have told them; on the example it gives 0→1; C{0,1,2}, Q{0,1}, where the instrument also shares randomness with the outcome, no gap. This is the `kept` predictor mode of the code ([`QmDAG.fritz_steps`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L787) splits each childful predictor before justifying and realising the step); `dropped` is the other.
* **Splitting the predicted node** (`copy` mode). Here $s$ is split and the deletion is applied to the copy $s'$, so the original keeps all of its parents while $s'$ keeps $\mathrm{common}(s)$. The copy must keep the children of $s$: the pull-back turns a classical model in which $s$ outputs a pair $(s_1,s_2)$ into a model with $s:=s_1$ and $s':=s_2$, and the children of $s$ may depend on $s_2$. Removing an edge $s'\to c$ would be an unjustified deletion; those edges can only go later, by a piggyback that justifies it. [`fritz_steps`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L787) literally calls [`split_node`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L548) first and then runs replace mode on the copy; the pair's shared facet is absorbed by the facet the copy shares with the predictors.

Both predicted-node modes (`replace`, `copy`) and both predictor modes are available to both certificates. The search has one trick, `Fritz`, whose transitions record the target, the deleted parents, the predictor set, the predicted-node mode, the predictor mode (`dropped` or `kept`) and the certificate, `dsep` when d-separation suffices and `entropic` when the LP of Section [7](#7-the-entropic-certificate-for-the-fritz-piggyback-qmdag_entropic_certificate) was needed (Section [9](#9-search-certificates-and-the-four-node-census-qc_gap_searchpy-special-applicationsproving_qc_gapspy)). Kept predictors reach strictly more (the KPC construction of [7.7](#77-worked-example-the-kpc-structure) keeps its childless predictor), but a kept-predictor output has as many visible nodes as its source, so an exhaustive closure under kept predictors is far larger than under dropped ones; the search applies the kept-predictor tricks at depth one, once to each input the cheaper stages leave unproven (Section [9](#9-search-certificates-and-the-four-node-census-qc_gap_searchpy-special-applicationsproving_qc_gapspy)). The cascade of [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade) separates the contributions of the predictor mode, the predicted-node mode and the certificate, and says where kept predictors prove something dropped predictors cannot.

### 6.3 Theorem (d-separation certificate)

**Claim.** Let $\mathbf X$ be a predictor set and $T$ a set of predicted nodes, each as in [6.1](#61-the-mechanism), and suppose that for every $s\in T$, in the effective DAG of $G$ (private noise nodes included),

```math
\mathbf X\setminus\mathrm{common}(s)\ \perp_d\ \mathrm{others}(s)\ \big|\ \mathrm{common}(s).
```

(A predictor that is itself a parent of $s$ lies in $\mathrm{common}(s)$ and is conditioned on rather than tested; if every predictor is a parent of $s$ the condition is vacuous.) Let $G'$ be the output of [6.1](#61-the-mechanism), every $s\in T$ restricted to $\mathrm{common}(s)$, with the predictors removed, deleted if childless and marginalized otherwise, in any order. Then a QC gap in $G'$ implies a QC gap in $G$. The same holds for the kept form of Section [6](#6-the-fritz-piggyback-fritz).2 (a childless predictor untouched; a childful one split, the copy being the predictor that is marginalized), by applying the statement to the split structure and composing with the node-splitting piggyback.

**Proof.** *Lift:* given $P'\in\mathcal Q(G')$, first lift it to the candidate $G_1$ through the removal of the predictors: a deleted predictor outputs a constant (Section [2](#2-point-distribution-pd)), a marginalized one runs the lift of Section [3](#3-marginalization-naive-and-with-teleportation-naive_marginalization-teleportation_marginalization) (it forwards its visible parents, broadcasts the classical strings and teleports its quantum shares to its children). This gives $P_1\in\mathcal Q(G_1)$ with $P'$ as its marginal over the predictors. In $P_1$ the parents of each $s\in T$ are classical and $s$ has a channel from a predictor, so $s$ can be realised as a deterministic function $f_s(\mathrm{common}(s))$ with its private randomness drawn from that channel. Now run $P_1$ in $G$ and let each predictor additionally output what it sees of every $f_s(\mathrm{common}(s))$. The result $P$ lies in $\mathcal Q(G)$, satisfies $H(s\mid\mathbf X)=0$ for every $s\in T$, and has $P'$ as the marginal over the predictors.
*Pull-back:* let $M$ be a classical model of $G$ for $P$. Take $s\in T$ and write $s=h(\mathrm{common},\mathrm{others})$ with $\mathrm{others}$ including the private noise, and $s=g(\mathbf X)$. Condition on $\mathrm{common}(s)$. By the d-separation hypothesis and (F2) for classical models, $\mathrm{others}(s)$ and $\mathbf X$ are independent given $\mathrm{common}(s)$. A random variable that is a function of each of two independent variables is almost surely constant, so $s=\varphi_s(\mathrm{common}(s))$ a.s. Replace the kernel of $s$ in $M$ by $\varphi_s$; the joint distribution is unchanged. Do this for each $s\in T$ in turn: every replacement leaves the joint distribution, hence the independences the next one uses, untouched, and the d-separation hypothesis of each $s$ is a statement about $G$, not about the structure left by the earlier replacements. The resulting model is Markov to $G_1$, the predictors still present with their fine-grained outputs. Now remove the predictors: marginalizing a node of a classical model gives a classical model of the latent projection (Section [3](#3-marginalization-naive-and-with-teleportation-naive_marginalization-teleportation_marginalization)), which is $G'$, for the marginal of $P$, which is $P'$; a childless predictor is simply deleted. Hence $P'\in\mathcal C(G')$. $\square$

The set test is equivalent to the conjunction of the per-edge tests $\mathbf X\perp_d v\mid\mathrm{Pa}(s)\setminus\lbrace v\rbrace$ over $v\in\mathrm{others}(s)$, by the weak-union and intersection properties of d-separation. The implementation is [`QmDAG.fritz_certificate`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L683), which short-circuits the vacuous case $\mathbf X\subseteq\mathrm{common}(s)$ and otherwise tests the set at once with networkx's `is_d_separator`; the candidate is built by [`QmDAG._restrict_target`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L752) from the kept set, and the predictors are removed by [`QmDAG.fritz_realise`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L769).

### 6.4 The unit: a predictor and its targets

The unit of the piggyback is a predictor set $\mathbf X$ together with a set $T$ of predicted nodes, its **targets**. Each target's deletion is read off the predictor: $s$ keeps $\mathrm{common}(s)$, what $\mathbf X$ sees, and loses $\mathrm{others}(s)$, everything $\mathbf X$ cannot see, all of it or nothing (Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks) states the procedure). Deleting a single parent $p\to s$ while $s$ keeps another parent $q$ that $\mathbf X$ cannot see is not a candidate: the predictors cannot predict $s$, and no other piggyback justifies the lone deletion. Example: 0→2, 1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}. Predictor 3 deletes 0→2 and 1→2 together and reaches Q{0,1}, C{2,3}, Q{1,3}, which degrades to [`QG_Bell_C_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L78) (an entropic step, [7.8](#78-further-examples-from-the-census)), whereas neither structure with only one of the two edges deleted is reachable from the input by any trick of the toolkit, out of 760 reachable structures. Across the four-node census, of the single-target Fritz steps that delete two or more non-noise parents, more than half have no reachable single-edge intermediate.

**Several targets at once.** One predictor may predict several nodes in the same step, each restricted to what the predictor sees of it; Theorem [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) is stated for a set $T$, and its pull-back replaces the kernels one at a time. This is Fritz's original argument: in the triangle, node 2 predicts copies of 0 and of 1 together, and when 2 is then dropped what is left is the Bell structure with classical settings (the first example of [6.5](#65-examples)). With the predictor *kept* and childless, a joint step is literally the sequence of its single-target steps, since the restrictions commute and the predictor is untouched. With the predictor *dropped*, it is not: after the first single-target step the predictor is gone and nothing predicts the second target, so the sequential route needs two kept-predictor steps followed by a point distribution, three steps in all, two of them Fritz steps. The search applies the Fritz trick at depth one ([9.2](#92-two-phases-eight-fritz-stages)), so for it the joint step is the only route to Bell from the triangle; and joint steps are cheap, one surgery per subset of the targets already certified singly and no LP, whereas a second Fritz depth means re-expanding every Fritz output under the trick, which was measured to cost more than everything else combined. The code therefore emits, besides the single targets, every set of two or more targets of one predictor that d-separation certifies ([8.6](#86-joint-target-sets-and-why-not-a-second-fritz-depth)); sets with an LP-certified member are not emitted, because the relabelled LP certificates ([7.3](#73-the-certificate-and-its-two-target-sets-qmdag_entropic_certificate)) are statements about different substitutions of the latents and do not obviously compose, while a *joint* LP, one candidate restricting all the targets and the `markov` target set, is a sound option that an earlier version of the code had and that never decided an input (Section [10](#10-open-questions)). Joint predictor *sets*, on the other hand, are not reducible to single predictors: a set sees more than any member (`max_predictors`, off in the census).

### 6.5 Examples

**The triangle, Fritz's original argument** (one joint step; the paradigm of the piggyback). Input: no edges; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, Q{1,2}. Predictor 2, targets 0 and 1 in copy mode, 2 dropped. Copy mode splits both targets first: 0 becomes 0 and 0′, 1 becomes 1 and 1′, and the facets follow the copies, Q{0,0′,1,1′}, Q{0,0′,2}, Q{1,1′,2}. Node 2 sees Q{0,0′,2} and Q{1,1′,2}, so the copy 0′ keeps Q{0,0′,2} and loses Q{0,0′,1,1′} and its noise, and 1′ likewise keeps Q{1,1′,2}. The d-separation test for 0′ asks whether 2 is separated from Q{0,0′,1,1′} and the noise of 0′ given Q{0,0′,2}: every path from 2 to that facet runs through 0 or 1 as a collider (2 ← Q{0,0′,2} → 0 ← Q{0,0′,1,1′}), so it holds, and the same for 1′. In the lift, 2 holds a share of each pair state; 0′ and 1′ are deterministic functions of their shares, which 2 announces: the perfect correlation. Worked out:

* `Fritz`, predictor 2, targets 0′ and 1′ (copy mode), 2 dropped. 0′ keeps Q{0,0′,2}, classical for it (C{0,0′,2}); 1′ keeps C{1,1′,2}; the originals keep Q{0,1}; the childless 2 is deleted and its shares vanish: C{0,0′}, C{1,1′}, Q{0,1}, which is [`QG_Bell_C_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L78): 0′ and 1′ are the settings, each shared classically with its measurement, and the two measurements share the entangled pair.

Read backwards, this is Fritz's observation: a Bell experiment whose settings are chosen by a third party sharing randomness with each measurement is a triangle. Both copies must be predicted in one step: with 2 dropped after predicting 0′ alone, nothing is left to predict 1′ ([6.4](#64-the-unit-a-predictor-and-its-targets)).

**Replace mode** (the one census input that needs a `Fritz` step in replace mode and nothing more). Input: no edges; Q{0,1,2}, Q{0,1,3}, Q{0,2,3}, Q{1,2,3}, every triple entangled. Predictor 0, predicted 1. The parents of 1 are the three facets containing it; 0 sees Q{0,1,2} and Q{0,1,3}, so $\mathrm{common}(1)$ is those two facets and $\mathrm{others}(1)$ is Q{1,2,3} plus the noise of 1. The d-separation test asks whether 0 is separated from Q{1,2,3} given the two common facets: every path from 0 to Q{1,2,3} runs through 2 or 3 as a collider (0 ← Q{0,2,3} → 2 ← Q{1,2,3}), so it holds. In the lift, 1 is a function of its two shares and 0, which holds the same shares, announces that function; classically, any model in which 0 predicts 1 perfectly cannot let 1 depend on Q{1,2,3}, which 0 never sees. Worked out:

* `Fritz`, predictor 0, predicted 1 in replace mode, 0 dropped. Node 1 keeps Q{0,1,2} and Q{0,1,3}, which become classical for it, and loses Q{1,2,3}; the childless 0 is deleted, so its shares vanish: C{1,2}, C{1,3}, Q{2,3}, which is [`QG_Triangle`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L42).

**Deleting a visible edge.** Nothing in the mechanism distinguishes a visible parent of $s$ from a facet: $\mathrm{Pa}(s)$ contains both, and a parent the predictor cannot see is deleted either way. Input: 0→1, 0→3; Q{1,2}, Q{2,3} (a census input, though one that node stitching already proves: stitching the sink 2 onto the source 0 gives [`QG_Evans`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L43)). Two Fritz routes delete the edge 0→1:

* **Route 1, replace mode, kept predictor.** Predictor 2, predicted 1. $\mathrm{Pa}(1)$ is the visible parent 0, the facet Q{1,2} and the noise of 1; 2 sees Q{1,2} and Q{2,3}, so $\mathrm{common}(1)$ is Q{1,2} and $\mathrm{others}(1)$ is the edge 0→1 and the noise. The d-separation test asks whether 2 is separated from 0 given Q{1,2}: the only path, 2 ← Q{1,2} → 1 ← 0, is blocked at the collider 1. Node 1 keeps Q{1,2}, classical for it, and loses 0→1; 2 is childless, so kept means untouched: 0→3; C{1,2}, Q{2,3}, which is [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80) (1 and 3 are the measurements, 2 and 0 their settings). The lift lets 1 be a function of its share of Q{1,2}, which 2 announces; classically, a model in which 2 predicts 1 perfectly cannot let 1 depend on 0, which 2 never sees.
* **Route 2, copy mode, dropped predictor.** Predictor 1, predicted 2 in copy mode: 2 is split into 2 and 2′ (Q{1,2,2′}, Q{2,2′,3}), and 1 sees Q{1,2,2′} only, so 2′ keeps that facet, classical for it, and loses its share of Q{2,2′,3}; the childless 1 is deleted. The output is 0→3; C{2,2′}, Q{2,3}, which is again [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80) with the roles of the parties exchanged. Here it is the *predictor*, not the target, that loses the edge: 1 is deleted and 0→1 with it.

Both routes are recorded by the search ([`tests/test_fritz.py`](../tests/test_fritz.py)). With the visible parents in the predictor pool (Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks)) a third one appears: 0 predicts 1 vacuously (0 is a parent of 1, so $s=g(0)$ is a function of $\mathrm{common}(1)$ outright), 1 keeps 0 alone and loses Q{1,2}, and in kept mode the copy 0′ that did the predicting is marginalized, which re-supplies the deleted edge as the classical facet C{0,1,3}: 0→3; C{0,1,3}, Q{2,3}, a structure without a gap. A parent predictor deletes what it cannot see and hands back what it can, so it rarely leads anywhere; the census confirms it never decides an input ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)).

**Copy mode** (one of three census inputs that need a copy-mode `Fritz` step; four need copy mode in some trick). Input: 0→2, 1→2, 2→3; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, Q{1,3}. Predictor 0 (childful: it feeds 2), predicted 1 in copy mode. $\mathrm{common}(1)$ is Q{0,1}; $\mathrm{others}(1)$ is Q{1,3} plus noise; 0 is separated from Q{1,3} given Q{0,1} because the paths through 1 and through 2 both end in colliders. The copy 1' keeps Q{0,1}, classically, and the child 2 of 1; the original 1 keeps Q{1,3}. Dropping 0 marginalizes it with teleportation (its share of Q{0,2} goes to 2, its facets become classical for 2). Replace mode cannot do this: it would strip Q{1,3} from 1, and that facet is the one that survives into the seed. Worked out:

* `Fritz`, predictor 0, predicted 1 in copy mode, 0 dropped. The copy 1′ keeps Q{0,1} (classical for it, as C{0,1,1′}) and the child 2; the original 1 keeps Q{1,3} and 2. Then 0 is marginalized with teleportation: its share of Q{0,1} is teleported to 2 through Q{0,2}, giving Q{1,2}; its classical facet is relayed to 2: 1→2→3, 1′→2; Q{1,2}, Q{1,3}, C{1,1′,2}.
* Teleportation marginalization of 1: its share of Q{1,3} is teleported to 2 through Q{1,2}: 1′→2→3; Q{2,3}, C{1′,2}, which is [`QG_Instrumental_EdgeC`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L40) (instrument 1′, treatment 2, outcome 3).

The other two inputs that need a copy-mode `Fritz` step are 0→1→2→3; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, Q{1,3} and 0→1, 0→2, 1→2, 2→3; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, Q{1,3}. Both take the same two steps with the same predictor and predicted node: the visible edges out of 0 are removed with 0 (a predictor that is a parent of $s$ lies in $\mathrm{common}(s)$, [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate)), so the structure after the `Fritz` step is the same 1→2→3, 1′→2; Q{1,2}, Q{1,3}, C{1,1′,2} in all three cases.

**Kept predictor** (the one census input first proven with a kept predictor by d-separation, in copy mode). Input: 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}, Q{1,3}. Predictor 0, which feeds 1, predicts 3 in copy mode: $\mathrm{common}(3)$ is Q{0,3}, $\mathrm{others}(3)$ is Q{1,3} and noise, separated from 0 given Q{0,3} because 0→1←Q{1,3} is blocked at 1. Since 0 has a child, it is split into 0 and a full copy 0′ (both feeding 1); the copy predicts and is marginalized. Worked out, with $s=3$ and $X=0$:

1. **Node splitting of the predicted node** (the copy-mode preprocessing, a piggyback of its own, [6.2](#62-node-splitting-and-the-two-predictor-modes-qmdagsplit_node)): 3 becomes 3 and 3′, same parents, no children: 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{0,3,3′}, Q{1,3,3′}.
2. **One Fritz step**, predictor 0, predicted 3′ in replace mode, predictor kept. The three operations below are bundled into this single step, and the structures after the first two are *not* QmDAGs in the sense of [0.1](#01-structures-and-model-sets): the one after the second operation carries the observational restriction that the subvariable 0′ is perfectly correlated with 3′ (the lift arranges it, and the pull-back of [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) uses its one direction, $H(3'\mid 0')=0$), so its model sets are not the unrestricted $\mathcal Q$ and $\mathcal C$ of its graph and no theorem of this note applies to it. Only the output of the third operation, with the correlated subvariable marginalized out, is a structure whose gap the piggyback asserts; pausing earlier would be unsound.
   1. Split the predictor 0 into 0 and a full copy 0′ (same facets, same child 1): 0→1→2, 0′→1; Q{0,0′,2}, Q{0,0′,3,3′}, Q{1,3,3′}. The copy 0′ is the subvariable that the lift correlates perfectly with 3′.
   2. Restrict 3′ to $\mathrm{common}(3')$: 3′ loses Q{1,3,3′}, which stays Q{1,3}, and keeps the facet shared with 0′, classical for it: 0→1→2, 0′→1; Q{0,0′,2}, Q{0,0′,3}, C{0,0′,3,3′}, Q{1,3}, with 0′ perfectly correlated with 3′. The d-separation of [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) holds since 0′→1←Q{1,3} is blocked at 1.
   3. Marginalize 0′ (no teleportation: 0′ and 1 share no quantum facet), relaying its facets to 1 classically: 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}, Q{1,3}, C{0,1,3,3′}, C{0,1,2}. The copy 3′ now has a single classical parent and no quantum one. This is the output of the Fritz step, and the first structure after the input whose gap follows.
3. **Conditioning** on 3 (no visible parents; every facet of 3 lies within its latent siblings 0, 1, 3′): 3 is removed, Q{0,3} and Q{1,3} swap entanglement onto 0 and 1: 0→1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, C{0,1,3′}, C{0,1,2}.
4. **Teleportation marginalization** of 0: its share of Q{0,2} is teleported to 1 through Q{0,1}, its classical facets lose 0: 1→2; Q{1,2}, C{1,3′}, which is [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41).

This is the route the census records for this input, at the fourth d-separation stage ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)). The input also has an LP route in replace mode: predictor 3 (childless, kept) predicts 1, which loses 0→1 and keeps Q{1,3} classically, giving 1→2; C{1,3}, Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}; marginalizing 1 (no quantum facet left, so plainly) gives C{2,3}, Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}, which degrades to [`QG_Triangle`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L42). The earlier cascade, which ran the LP within each pair of modes, recorded that one; with every d-separation stage ahead of every LP stage the cheaper certificate wins.

The order of steps 3 and 4 cannot be swapped. Before the conditioning, 0 and 1 share no quantum facet, so marginalizing 0 first cannot teleport its share of Q{0,2}; that share is relayed to 1 as a classical common cause only, leaving 1→2; Q{1,3}, C{1,2}, C{1,3,3′}, and conditioning on 3 then swaps nothing, since 3 has a single quantum sibling left, and leaves 1→2; C{1,2}, C{1,3′}, a structure with no quantum facet and hence no gap. The entanglement between 0 and 1 that the teleportation needs exists only after the conditioning has swapped it there. With 0 dropped instead of split, 0's facets land on 1 classically from the start and the same quantum link is lost.

The soundness of such a composition does not rest on the lifts of the steps being one protocol: each step is the implication "gap in the output implies gap in the input", and the chain is a chain of implications. In the lift direction the steps are applied from the seed upwards, each to the distribution the previous one produced: the conditioning lift makes 3 perform a Bell measurement on its two shares, and the Fritz lift then re-implements only 3′ and 0′, leaving 3's strategy untouched; 3′ depends on a classical facet alone, so it is a deterministic function of it with randomness from that facet, and 0′ announces that function. The split lift merges 3 and 3′ into one node of the input whose output is the pair, Bell outcome and predicted value; 0 predicts the second component only. In the pull-back direction, which is the proof of the gap, each step takes an arbitrary classical model of its input and produces one of its output, using nothing about how the lifts were built.


## 7. The entropic certificate for the Fritz piggyback (`QmDAG._entropic_certificate`)

### 7.1 Why the same mechanism admits a stronger certificate

In the pull-back of [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) the only facts about the classical model that were used were the Markov property of $G$ and $H(s\mid\mathbf X)=0$. But the lifted distribution $P$ has more structure: it lies in $\mathcal Q(G_1)$, so by (F2) **every observable d-separation of $G_1$ holds for $P$**, and therefore holds in any classical model of $G$ for $P$. These conditional independences are properties of the *new* graph, obtained for free, and there is no reason not to use them as hypotheses when deciding whether a deletion is justified in the *original* graph. The d-separation test of [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) ignores them; it is a shortcut that trades proof power for speed. Khanna, Pusey and Colbeck's six-node example ([7.7](#77-worked-example-the-kpc-structure)) is a case where the shortcut fails and the extra independences are exactly what is needed.

### 7.2 Entropy vectors as the proof system (`entropic_lp.py`)

For $n$ random variables the entropy vector $h\in\mathbb R^{2^n-1}$ lists $H(S)$ for every nonempty $S$. Every entropy vector satisfies the **elemental Shannon inequalities** $H(i\mid[n]\setminus i)\ge0$ and $I(i{:}j\mid K)\ge0$ for $i\lt j$ and $K\subseteq[n]\setminus\lbrace i,j\rbrace$, which number $n+\binom n2 2^{n-2}$ ([`elemental_inequalities(n)`](../entropic_lp.py#L100); the $n=3$ matrix is checked against the Mathematica notebook of Khanna, Pusey and Colbeck (KPC), the row count for $n\le8$). A conditional independence is the vanishing of a conditional mutual information, a functional dependence the vanishing of a conditional entropy; both are nonnegative on the cone, so each hypothesis is one inequality "$\le0$". The local Markov property of a DAG is one conditional mutual information per node, $I(v{:}\mathrm{nondesc}(v)\setminus\mathrm{pa}(v)\mid\mathrm{pa}(v))=0$ ([`local_markov_rows`](../entropic_lp.py#L141)). The Shannon cone with these hypotheses implies exactly the d-separations of the DAG: every d-separation follows from the local Markov statements by the semigraphoid axioms (Verma and Pearl), which are Shannon-derivable, and nothing beyond the d-separations can follow because classical models realise every non-d-separated dependence (F2). This is tested on random DAGs.

A target functional $t$ is **implied** when the LP $\lbrace\text{Shannon}\ge0,\ \text{hypotheses}\le0,\ t\ge1\rbrace$ is infeasible (the cone is scale invariant). Geometrically the question is **cone inclusion**: the hypotheses cut out a polyhedral cone $K$, each target is a halfspace, and the certificate asks whether $K$ lies in the intersection of the target halfspaces, that is, inside the cone of entropy vectors Markov to the target graph. Both cones are given by inequalities, and inclusion of one inequality description in another needs no conversion to extreme rays (as cdd, lrs or PANDA would do; that is exponential and unnecessary). It needs one LP per target set, not one per target: every target is a conditional mutual information, nonnegative on the Shannon cone, and a sum of nonnegative quantities vanishes iff each term does, so all targets are implied iff their **sum** is implied ([`EntropicLP.implies_all`](../entropic_lp.py#L311), [`sum_rows`](../entropic_lp.py#L74)). The Farkas certificate of the summed row certifies every target at once. Solving one LP in place of $n$ per target set is where the time of the entropic stage goes, so this is roughly an $n$-fold saving. Infeasibility is decided with the Mosek Optimizer API ([`EntropicLP`](../entropic_lp.py#L195), interior point); the dual ray is a Farkas certificate, a nonnegative combination of elemental inequalities and hypotheses that reproduces $t$, available through [`EntropicLP.farkas_certificate`](../entropic_lp.py#L292). Implications proven this way are valid for every distribution, with or without full support, because they use only Shannon inequalities. The method is incomplete in the other direction: a feasible LP does not exhibit a distribution, only a vector in the Shannon cone.

### 7.3 The certificate and its two target sets (`QmDAG._entropic_certificate`)

Here $s$ is the predicted node and $\mathbf X$ the predicting set. Variables: all nodes of [`lp_structure`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L487), the visible nodes and the facets of $G$; quantum facets are ordinary latent variables here, since only classical models are analysed (F2). Hypotheses: Shannon; local Markov of $G$; $H(s\mid\mathbf X)\le0$; and $\mathcal I(G_1)$, the set of elementary observable d-separations $I(x{:}y\mid Z)=0$ of the candidate $G_1$ over its visible nodes ([`observable_dseparation_rows`](../entropic_lp.py#L159)). Two target sets are tried, each as one LP on its summed row ([7.2](#72-entropy-vectors-as-the-proof-system-entropic_lppy)), in this order: `relabel` first, since it is the certificate that reaches beyond d-separation in practice ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)), and `markov` only when `relabel` is inapplicable or fails.

* **`relabel`**: applicable when there is a single predicted node $s$ and it keeps exactly one parent in $G_1$, a facet $L$ (the code tests [`len(predicted) == 1`](../adjmat_class.py#L48), [`len(common) == 1`](../adjmat_class.py#L48) and that the element is a facet index). Let $G''$ be $G_1$ with $L$ deleted and $s$ made a parent of every other child of $L$. Targets: the local Markov equalities of $G''$ over $G$'s variables other than $L$.
* **`markov`**: the local Markov equalities of $G_1$ over $G$'s own latents. If all are implied, the joint of any classical model of $G$ satisfying the hypotheses is Markov to $G_1$, hence $P\in\mathcal C(G_1)$.

In the search ([9.2](#92-two-phases-eight-fritz-stages)) the d-separation test of [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) is run first on every candidate and the LP only where it fails, so a transition's certificate, `dsep` or `entropic`, says which justification the step needs. The census runs the LP with the `relabel` target set only, since `markov` never decided an input ([9.7](#97-entropic-certificates-attempted)); [`default_stages(lp_markov_target=True)`](../qc_gap_search.py#L111) turns it back on. Every step predicts a single node ([6.4](#64-the-unit-a-predictor-and-its-targets)).

### 7.4 Why `relabel` is justified

The claim to be proven is not about one classical model. It is: *every* classical model of $G$ whose observed statistics satisfy the perfect prediction and the independences $\mathcal I(G_1)$ can be exchanged for a classical model of $G_1$ with the same observed statistics. The argument has three steps.

1. **Fix one model.** Any classical model $M$ of $G$ producing $P$ defines one joint distribution $Q$ over the visible nodes and the facet latents (noises integrated out). The hypotheses are facts about $Q$: $Q$ is Markov to $G$; $H(s\mid\mathbf X)=0$; the independences of $\mathcal I(G_1)$ hold among the visible nodes. Shannon's inequalities hold for $Q$, so the entropy vector of $Q$ satisfies every LP hypothesis.
2. **What infeasibility proves.** The LP shows that every entropy vector satisfying the hypotheses has each target conditional mutual information equal to zero. A vanishing conditional mutual information is a conditional independence. So the targets hold for $Q$, and since $M$ was arbitrary they hold for every model satisfying the hypotheses.
3. **From Markov($G''$) to a model of $G_1$.** The targets are the local Markov conditions of $G''$ over $W=V\cup(\text{facets}\setminus\lbrace L\rbrace)$. For a DAG, the local Markov conditions are equivalent to the factorisation of $Q(W)$ into one kernel per node given its parents in $G''$, so the marginal of $Q$ over $W$ *is* a classical model of $G''$, one in which $s$ is a root. Now rename. Introduce a fresh latent $\lambda'$ carrying the value of $s$, with the root distribution $Q(s)$; let $s$ read $\lambda'$ deterministically (its private noise in $G_1$ may be trivial); let every other former child $c$ of $L$ keep its $G''$ kernel with $s$ replaced by $\lambda'$, which is legal because the parents of $c$ in $G_1$ are its parents in $G''$ with $s$ replaced by $L$; the kept quantum part of the facet over $F\setminus\lbrace s\rbrace$ is left unused; everything else is unchanged. This is a classical model of $G_1$ with observed marginal $Q(V)=P$. The original latent $\lambda_L$ of $M$ is simply discarded: in the new model the common cause of $s$ and its siblings is played by the value of $s$ itself.

In the KPC example ([7.7](#77-worked-example-the-kpc-structure)) this reads: every model in which $E$ depends on $C$ and $D$, with $F$ predicting $E$ and $E$ independent of $(C,D)$, is replaced by a model in which $E$ depends on a single latent $A'$ alone, where $A'$ is the old value of $E$.

**Why `markov` cannot do this.** The model $E=A\oplus B$, $D=C\oplus B$, $F=(A,B)$ satisfies every hypothesis of the KPC candidate, yet $E$ is not a function of the given $A$. No certificate that keeps $A$ as the witness can succeed; `relabel` succeeds because it discards $A$.

**Why the single-parent condition.** If $s$ kept another parent $v$ in $G_1$, then $s$ would not be a root of $G''$, and $\lambda':=s$ would be correlated with $v$, violating the independence of roots in $G_1$.

**Neither target set implies the other.** `relabel` without `markov` is the KPC example. `markov` without `relabel`: let $s$ read a visible parent $v$ and one facet $L=\lbrace s,c,d,x\rbrace$ shared with the predictor $x$. d-separation certifies deleting $v\to s$, so `markov` holds, but the `relabel` targets contain $d\perp c\mid s$, which the model $L=(L_1,L_2)$, $s=L_1$, $c=d=L_2$, $x=L$ violates with $I(d{:}c\mid s)=1$ bit while satisfying every hypothesis ([`tests/test_fritz_entropic.py`](../tests/test_fritz_entropic.py)). Both target sets are instances of one scheme, replace each latent of $G_1$ by a set of $G$'s variables and ask for Markov($G_1$) with the substitution: `markov` substitutes $\lbrace L\rbrace$, `relabel` substitutes $\lbrace s\rbrace$. Further substitutions are an open direction (Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks)).

**Relation to the d-separation certificate.** The pull-back of [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) derives $s\perp\mathrm{others}\mid\mathrm{common}$ and keeps the given latents, so Theorem [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) always produces a `markov`-type witness and never a relabelling. The LP subsumes it at least for the row of $s$: if $\mathbf X\perp_d\mathrm{others}\mid\mathrm{common}$ then the Shannon cone with the Markov hypotheses derives $I(\mathbf X{:}\mathrm{others}\mid\mathrm{common})\le0$, and with $H(s\mid\mathbf X)\le0$ and the Markov row of $s$ it derives $H(s\mid\mathrm{common})\le I(s{:}\mathbf X\mid\mathrm{common})\le I(\mathrm{others}{:}\mathbf X\mid\mathrm{common})+I(s{:}\mathbf X\mid\mathrm{common},\mathrm{others})=0$, which implies the `markov` row of $s$. The `markov` rows of other nodes, whose non-descendant sets grow in $G_1$, were checked empirically (every d-separation-admissible pair in the test structures is LP-admissible); the search never relies on this, since d-separation-certified candidates are admitted by Theorem [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) without an LP. `relabel` has no d-separation analogue in $G$: its hypotheses $\mathcal I(G_1)$ are facts about $G_1$, not about the graph of $G$.

### 7.5 Theorem (entropic certificate)

**Claim.** Let $G_1$ be a candidate as in [6.1](#61-the-mechanism), possibly with further parents deleted from any non-predictor node (the extra deletions of Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks)), and $G'$ the output with the predictors removed as in [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate). If the LP of [7.3](#73-the-certificate-and-its-two-target-sets-qmdag_entropic_certificate) implies the `markov` targets, or the single-parent condition holds and it implies the `relabel` targets, then a QC gap in $G'$ implies a QC gap in $G$.

**Proof.** Lift as in [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate): $P\in\mathcal Q(G)$ with $H(s\mid\mathbf X)=0$; since the lifted strategy is a strategy for $G_1$ with fine-grained predictor outputs, (F2) gives that $P$ satisfies every observable d-separation of $G_1$. Let $M$ be a classical model of $G$ for $P$. Its joint entropy vector lies in the Shannon cone, satisfies the local Markov equalities of $G$ and the hypotheses $H(s\mid\mathbf X)=0$ and $\mathcal I(G_1)$. The LP implication forces the target quantities to vanish, so the joint of $M$ is Markov to the restricted structure with the predictors present (or to its relabelled version, and then [7.4](#74-why-relabel-is-justified) gives such a model). Removing the predictors as in [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate), by deletion or marginalization, gives $P'\in\mathcal C(G')$. $\square$

Several predicted nodes for the same $\mathbf X$ could be handled jointly by the LP, with one hypothesis $H(s_i\mid\mathbf X)\le0$ per node, one candidate $G_1$ restricting all of them, and only the `markov` target set (the `relabel` substitution is per target and the relabelled certificates of two targets do not obviously compose). The code certifies joint target sets by d-separation only ([6.4](#64-the-unit-a-predictor-and-its-targets), [8.6](#86-joint-target-sets-and-why-not-a-second-fritz-depth)); an earlier version that ran the joint LP found it never decisive, and it is listed in Section [10](#10-open-questions).

### 7.6 LP size and time

Here $s$ is the predicted node and $X$ the predictor, as in Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks). An LP over $n$ variables (visible nodes plus facets, after any splitting) has $2^n-1$ columns and $n+\binom n2 2^{n-2}$ elemental rows; measured solve times on one core are about 0.2, 0.5, 2 and 9 seconds for $n=10,11,12,13$, and memory stays below a gigabyte up to $n=13$. The number of variables is not capped. Instead every solve carries a Mosek time limit of 60 seconds ([`EntropicLP(max_time=60.0)`](../entropic_lp.py#L195), `optimizer_max_time`); a solve that hits it is counted ([`entropic_lp.TIMEOUTS`](../entropic_lp.py#L85)) and treated as undecided, which is the conservative answer: the implication is then not claimed. The census report states how many solves timed out ([9.7](#97-entropic-certificates-attempted)).

### 7.7 Worked example: the KPC structure

$G_1$: visible $C,D,E,F$; facets $A=\lbrace E,F\rbrace$, $B=\lbrace D,F\rbrace$; edges $C\to D$, $C\to E$, $D\to E$. Predictor $F$, predicted $E$: $\mathrm{common}(E)=\lbrace A\rbrace$, $\mathrm{others}(E)=\lbrace C,D,\text{noise}\rbrace$. The d-separation test fails, since $F\leftarrow B\to D$ is open. The candidate $G_1$ has $E$ with parent $A$ only and d-separates $E$ from $\lbrace C,D\rbrace$. With the hypothesis $E\perp CD$ the LP certifies the `relabel` targets ($A:=E$), reproducing KPC's Lemma 1 without the equality $F_S=E$: their split node $F_S$ is exactly the childless copy that keeping the childless predictor $F$ implicitly deletes (Section [6](#6-the-fritz-piggyback-fritz).2), so keeping $F$ is sound. With $F$ kept the output is C→D; Q{D,F}, C{E,F}: the Bell variant [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80), in one step ([`tests/test_fritz_entropic.py`](../tests/test_fritz_entropic.py)). With $F$ dropped, $E$ is left with a private facet only and nothing is learnt: this is a case where kept predictors are essential. Worked out, with $s=E$ and $X=F$:

* Candidate $G_1$: $E$ keeps $A$ only (classical for it) and loses $C\to E$, $D\to E$ and its noise: C→D; Q{D,F}, C{E,F}, with $C$ and $D$ as before. Observable d-separations of $G_1$: $E\perp CD$.
* LP: from Markov($G$), $H(E\mid F)=0$ and $E\perp CD$, the `relabel` targets, Markov($G''$) with $E$ a root and a parent of $F$, are implied. Output, $F$ kept because it is childless: C→D; Q{D,F}, C{E,F}, which is [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80).

### 7.8 Further examples from the census

Six inputs have an LP certificate with a kept predictor in replace mode, and no input has one at any other LP stage; five of them are proven only this way, at the second LP stage of the cascade ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)), while the sixth, the kept-predictor example of [6.5](#65-examples), also has a d-separation route in copy mode, which the census records since every d-separation stage runs first. All six use a childless predictor $X=3$ that is kept and the `relabel` target set for the predicted node $s$. Keeping a predictor always means the split-and-marginalize of [6.2](#62-node-splitting-and-the-two-predictor-modes-qmdagsplit_node): $X$ is split into $X$ and a full copy $X'$, the copy carries the subvariable perfectly correlated with $s$, and the copy is removed, by deletion when childless and by marginalization otherwise. In every example below $X=3$ is childless, so the copy is simply deleted and the operation leaves no trace in the output; the examples look as if 3 were kept untouched, which is exactly the childless case of [6.2](#62-node-splitting-and-the-two-predictor-modes-qmdagsplit_node). In the four lines of each example, "facts" lists what the LP is given beyond Markov($G$): the perfect correlation arranged by the lift, in the one-directional form $H(s\mid X)=0$ that the LP uses, and *all* observable conditional independences of the candidate $G_1$, read off $G_1$ by d-separation. The proof uses all of them: every one holds for the lifted distribution, so there is nothing to gain by omitting any, and a minimal subset plays no role in the certificate. The independence marked "decisive" is a diagnostic, found afterwards by re-solving the LP with the prediction and one independence at a time, and is there only to show which fact carries the inference; the others are then consequences, since the targets imply every observable independence of the output. The last line says what the step deletes and which facet becomes classical for $s$.

* **Input** 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}. **Output** 0→1; C{2,3}, Q{1,3}, which is a relabelling of the seed [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80) (party 1 with setting 0, party 3 whose "setting" 2 is a classical copy correlated with it). **Facts** $H(2\mid3)=0$; $G$ itself has only $0\perp3$; the candidate $G_1$ adds $2\perp0$, $2\perp0\mid1$, $2\perp0\mid3$, $2\perp1$, $2\perp1\mid0$ and $3\perp0\mid2$, of which $2\perp1\mid0$ alone, with the prediction, implies the `relabel` targets; the prediction alone does not. **Step** $s=2$ loses the edge 1→2 and the facet Q{0,2}, which shrinks to nothing on 0; Q{2,3} stays quantum on 3 and becomes classical for 2, C{2,3}; d-separation fails because 3←Q{1,3}→1→2 is open.
* **Input** 0→2, 1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}. **Output** Q{0,1}, C{2,3}, Q{1,3}, which degrades (Q{0,1} made classical) to the seed [`QG_Bell_C_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L78). **Facts** $H(2\mid3)=0$; $G$ has only $0\perp3$; $G_1$ adds the same six independences as above, and again $2\perp1\mid0$ is the decisive one. **Step** $s=2$ loses both visible parents 0→2 and 1→2 at once ([6.4](#64-the-unit-a-predictor-and-its-targets)); Q{2,3} becomes classical for 2; no facet shrinks; d-separation fails through 3←Q{1,3}→1→2.
* **Input** 0→1, 0→2, 1→2; Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}. **Output** 0→1; C{2,3}, Q{1,3}, which is [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80). **Facts** $H(2\mid3)=0$; $G$ has only $0\perp3$; $G_1$ adds the same six independences, $2\perp1\mid0$ decisive. **Step** $s=2$ loses 0→2 and 1→2; Q{2,3} becomes classical for 2; no facet shrinks; d-separation fails through 3←Q{1,3}→1→2.
* **Input** 0→1, 0→2, 1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}. **Output** 0→1; Q{0,1}, C{2,3}, Q{1,3}, which degrades (Q{0,1} made classical) to a relabelling of the seed [`QG_Bell_C_EdgeC`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L79). **Facts** as in the previous example: $H(2\mid3)=0$, $0\perp3$ from $G$, the six new independences of $G_1$, $2\perp1\mid0$ decisive. **Step** $s=2$ loses 0→2 and 1→2; Q{2,3} becomes classical for 2; no facet shrinks; d-separation fails through 3←Q{1,3}→1→2 and through 3←Q{1,3}→1←0→2.
* **Input** 0→1, 0→2, 1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}. **Output** 0→1; C{2,3}, Q{1,3}, which is [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80). **Facts** as above, $2\perp1\mid0$ decisive. **Step** $s=2$ loses 0→2, 1→2 and the facet Q{0,2}, which shrinks to nothing on 0; Q{2,3} becomes classical for 2; d-separation fails through 3←Q{1,3}→1→2.
* **Input** 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}, Q{1,3}. **Output** 1→2; C{1,3}, Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}; marginalizing 1 (no quantum facet left on it) gives C{2,3}, Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}, which degrades to the seed [`QG_Triangle`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L42). **Facts** $H(1\mid3)=0$; $G$ has no observable independence at all; $G_1$ adds $1\perp0$ and $2\perp3\mid1$, of which $1\perp0$ alone, with the prediction, implies the `relabel` targets; $2\perp3\mid1$ plays no role. **Step** $s=1$ loses the edge 0→1; Q{1,3} becomes classical for 1; no facet shrinks; d-separation fails because 3←Q{0,3}→0→1 is open. This is the kept-predictor example of [6.5](#65-examples), whose d-separation proof needs copy mode and a childful predictor; the LP proves it with the childless predictor 3 instead.

Dropping the predictor instead would delete 3 and with it the only facet $s$ keeps, proving nothing, so these inputs are exactly where kept predictors matter for the LP ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)). The `markov` target set, the joint LP targets and the extra deletions of earlier versions of the code certified many candidate steps and were never decisive in four nodes; of the three only `markov` survives, as an option that is off ([9.7](#97-entropic-certificates-attempted)), and the other two are recorded as open questions (Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks)).

## 8. The Fritz search procedure (`QmDAG.fritz_steps`, `qc_gap_search.fritz_tricks`)

This section is self-contained. Throughout, $\mathbf X$ is the predictor set (a single node $X$ in the census), $s$ a predicted node, called a **target** in the code, and parents are parents in the effective DAG: the visible parents of a node, the facets containing it, and its private noise. The procedure is written in a few short methods of [`QmDAG`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L66) that are read top to bottom ([`fritz_pool`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L637), [`fritz_targets`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L709), [`fritz_deletion`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L661), [`fritz_certificate`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L683), [`fritz_realise`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L769), [`fritz_steps`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L787)), so that any Fritz step in a certificate can be re-derived without leaving the class.

### 8.1 The unit: pick a predictor, then read off the deletion at each of its targets

The search does not choose edges. It picks a predictor set $\mathbf X$, then runs through the candidate targets of $\mathbf X$ in a fixed order ([8.4](#84-the-pool-and-its-order-qmdagfritz_pool-qmdagfritz_targets)); at each target the parents to delete are *read off* the predictor, and the only question left is whether that deletion is justified ([8.3](#83-justifying-the-deletion-one-set-two-certificates-qmdagfritz_certificate)). Nothing is enumerated over subsets of parents: for one $(\mathbf X, s)$ there is exactly one candidate deletion, and if it is not justified the search moves to the next target, not to a smaller deletion. Once the targets of $\mathbf X$ are settled singly, every set of two or more of those that d-separation certified is emitted as one joint step ([8.6](#86-joint-target-sets-and-why-not-a-second-fritz-depth)), which is how Fritz's triangle reaches Bell.

### 8.2 What a predictor sees, and the deletion it dictates (`QmDAG.fritz_deletion`)

A predictor $x$ **sees** itself and its parents: its visible parents, the facets it belongs to, and its own noise. A set sees the union, $\mathrm{seen}(\mathbf X)=\mathbf X\cup\mathrm{Pa}(\mathbf X)$. The parents of $s$ split accordingly:

```math
K=\mathrm{common}(s)=\mathrm{Pa}(s)\cap\mathrm{seen}(\mathbf X),\qquad D=\mathrm{others}(s)=\mathrm{Pa}(s)\setminus K .
```

$K$ is what $s$ keeps, $D$ is what is deleted, and $D$ always contains the noise of $s$, which no other node sees. Both sets are fixed by $(\mathbf X, s)$ alone, before any test is run; they are the `common` and `others` of the old code and of [6.1](#61-the-mechanism). Two degenerate outcomes end the pair at once: $K$ holds no **channel** from a predictor to $s$ (no facet containing both, no edge $X\to s$), in which case the lift cannot give $s$ its randomness through $\mathbf X$ and the pair is unsound as a predictor-target pair; or $D$ is the noise of $s$ and nothing else, in which case the step would restrict $s$ to all of its parents, a no-op whose only effect, removing the predictors, is what marginalization and point distribution already do. Everything else is a **candidate**: $s$ restricted to $K$, with $D$ gone.

Three readings of the same rule, in the examples used below (Q{…} names the facet node of the effective DAG):

* **Tetrahedron**, no edges; Q{0,1,2}, Q{0,1,3}, Q{0,2,3}, Q{1,2,3}; target 1, predictor 0. $\mathrm{seen}(0)$ is 0 and its facets Q{0,1,2}, Q{0,1,3}, Q{0,2,3}. $\mathrm{Pa}(1)$ is Q{0,1,2}, Q{0,1,3}, Q{1,2,3} and the noise of 1. So $K$ = {Q{0,1,2}, Q{0,1,3}} and $D$ = {Q{1,2,3}, noise}.
* **KPC** ([7.7](#77-worked-example-the-kpc-structure)), 0→1, 0→2, 1→2; Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}; target 2, predictor 3. $\mathrm{seen}(3)$ is 3, Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}. $\mathrm{Pa}(2)$ is the visible parents 0 and 1, the facet Q{2,3} and the noise. So $K$ = {Q{2,3}} and $D$ = {0→2, 1→2, noise}: two visible edges go, because 3 sees neither 0 nor 1.
* **A pair that fails**, 1→3; Q{0,1,3}, Q{1,2}, Q{2,3} (a census input still unproven, Section [10](#10-open-questions)); target 2, predictor 3. $\mathrm{seen}(3)$ is 3, its visible parent 1, and Q{0,1,3}, Q{2,3}. $\mathrm{Pa}(2)$ is Q{1,2}, Q{2,3} and the noise. So $K$ = {Q{2,3}} and $D$ = {Q{1,2}, noise}.

In all three the deletion is settled. Whether it is *justified* is the next question, and it is a different question. (The copy mode of [6.2](#62-node-splitting-and-the-two-predictor-modes-qmdagsplit_node) changes nothing in the rule: the target is then the copy $s'$, which has the parents of $s$, and the predictor sees the facets with the copy added, so for the triangle with 0 and 1 split, 2 sees Q{0,0′,2} and Q{1,1′,2} and the copy 0′ keeps Q{0,0′,2}, [6.5](#65-examples).)

### 8.3 Justifying the deletion: one set, two certificates (`QmDAG.fritz_certificate`)

The candidate is sound iff every classical model of $G$ in which $\mathbf X$ predicts $s$ perfectly ($H(s\mid\mathbf X)=0$) and which satisfies the observable independences of the candidate is a classical model of the candidate ([6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) and [7.5](#75-theorem-entropic-certificate)). Two arguments can establish it, and both are asked about the *same* $D$:

1. **d-separation**: $\mathbf X\setminus K\ \perp_d\ D\ \big|\ K$ in the effective DAG, noise included. When it holds the pull-back is the elementary argument of [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate): conditioned on $K$, $s$ is a function of $\mathbf X$ and a function of $D$, which are independent, hence constant. Certificate `dsep`.
2. **the entropic LP** of Section [7](#7-the-entropic-certificate-for-the-fritz-piggyback-qmdag_entropic_certificate), run only when d-separation fails, with the same kept set $K$ as its candidate. Certificate `entropic`.

The point that is easy to misread: a failed d-separation test does **not** mean that the set to delete is empty. The set is $D$, fixed in [8.2](#82-what-a-predictor-sees-and-the-deletion-it-dictates-qmdagfritz_deletion); the failure means that the elementary argument cannot justify deleting it, and the LP is asked to justify deleting exactly it. In the old code, "admissible targets" were the targets whose d-separation test passed; an inadmissible target was a pair with no step, never a pair with a smaller deletion.

* **Tetrahedron**: is 0 separated from {Q{1,2,3}, noise of 1} given {Q{0,1,2}, Q{0,1,3}}? Every path from 0 to Q{1,2,3} passes through 2 or 3 as a collider (0 ← Q{0,2,3} → 2 ← Q{1,2,3}), and the noise of 1 reaches 0 only through 1, a collider on that path too. Yes: `dsep`. The output is [`QG_Triangle`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L42).
* **KPC**: is 3 separated from {0, 1, noise of 2} given Q{2,3}? No: 3 ← Q{1,3} → 1 is open. d-separation cannot justify deleting 0→2 and 1→2. The LP, given $H(2\mid 3)=0$, the Markov conditions of $G$ and the observable independences of the candidate (2 ⊥ {0,1}), certifies the `relabel` target set ([7.4](#74-why-relabel-is-justified)): `entropic`. The deletion is the same {0→2, 1→2}; only the argument changed. The output with 3 kept is [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80).
* **The failing pair**: is 3 separated from {Q{1,2}, noise of 2} given Q{2,3}? No: 3 ← 1 ← Q{1,2} is open, 3 reads 1 and 1 reads the facet. The LP fails as well, and here the failure is a true negative: the model $L_{12}$ uniform, $1=L_{12}$, $2=L_{12}$, $3=1$ is a classical model of $G$ in which 3 predicts 2 perfectly, yet 2 depends on Q{1,2} and not on Q{2,3}, so it is not a model of the candidate, and no certificate can exist. The search moves on to the next predictor of 2.

### 8.4 The pool and its order (`QmDAG.fritz_pool`, `QmDAG.fritz_targets`)

**Who may predict whom.** The lift must hand $s$ its private randomness through a channel the predictor also sees ([6.1](#61-the-mechanism)): a facet shared with $s$ (a latent sibling) or the edge $X\to s$ (a visible parent). A node with neither channel cannot predict $s$ for an arbitrary $P'$, since it would have to reproduce randomness it has no access to, so the pool of sound predictors is exactly *latent siblings $\cup$ visible parents*, and nothing wider. This is narrower than the Markov blanket of $s$, which also holds the children of $s$ and the other parents of those children and of the siblings: a co-parent of a child of $s$ sees nothing of $s$'s inputs and is unsound as a predictor, not merely unhelpful. The census pool is the latent siblings (`pool='siblings'`); the visible parents are an option (`pool='siblings+parents'`) that proves nothing further in four nodes ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade), [8.5](#85-parent-predictors-why-the-certificate-is-vacuous)). [`fritz_pool(s)`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L637) lists the predictors of a target with this bound; [`fritz_targets(X)`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L709) inverts it, listing the nodes whose pool contains the predictor, and is what the predictor-first loop iterates over.

**Order.** Predictors by index. The targets of a predictor: nodes sharing more facets with it first, then by index (so that, within the pool, latent siblings come before mere parents). Joint predictor sets, which see the union of what their members see, are enumerated up to `max_predictors` (1 in the census); a set is tried at a target only when every member lies in the target's pool, each with its own channel.

**Descendants.** A descendant $x$ of $s$ fails the d-separation test for every $K$: the noise of $s$ lies in $D$, and the path noise → $s$ → … → $x$ is open given any subset of $\mathrm{Pa}(s)$. The search therefore removes descendants from the pool by default (`allow_descendants=False`). They are nevertheless *sound* predictors when they have a channel: the lift of [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) applies verbatim, and the pull-back of [7.5](#75-theorem-entropic-certificate) is a statement about every classical model satisfying the hypotheses, in which the noise plays no role. The census experiment with `--descendants` ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)) finds six candidate steps that only a descendant certifies, on three inputs, all with the same target and predictor:

| input | target | predictor | deleted | output with the predictor kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0→1→2→3; Q{0,2}, Q{1,3} | 1 | 3 | 0→1 | 1→2→3; C{1,3}, Q{0,2} |
| 0→1, 0→2, 1→2, 2→3; Q{0,2}, Q{1,3} | 1 | 3 | 0→1 | 0→2, 1→2, 2→3; C{1,3}, Q{0,2} |
| 0→1, 0→2, 1→2, 2→3; Q{1,3} | 1 | 3 | 0→1 | 0→2, 1→2, 2→3; C{1,3} |

(the other three are the same steps with 3 dropped, which deletes 3 and leaves three-node outputs). In each, 3 is a descendant of 1 through 1→2→3, $\mathrm{seen}(3)$ is 3, its parent 2 and Q{1,3}, so $K$ = {Q{1,3}} and $D$ = {0→1, noise of 1}. d-separation fails because the noise of 1 reaches 3 along 1→2→3. The LP does not model that path as a leak: its hypotheses are $H(1\mid 3)=0$, the Markov conditions of $G$ and the observable independence 1 ⊥ 0 of the candidate (in the candidate, 1 reads Q{1,3} only), and from these it certifies the `relabel` targets, Markov($G_1$) with 1 in the role of Q{1,3}. Informally, d-separation insists that the predictor's knowledge of $s$ arrive through the shared facet, while the LP only uses *that* 3 knows 1, together with the independences, not *how*; here 3 learns 1 through the visible path and that is enough. These are six more certified implications, not new gaps: none of the three outputs is a known gap as far as the seeds know (none reaches a seed under the elementary tricks), so the three inputs stay unproven, and the ladder is unchanged. The cost is real: every descendant candidate goes to the LP, 1306 solves against 714.

### 8.5 Parent predictors: why the certificate is vacuous

Let $X$ be a visible parent of $s$ (with or without a shared facet). Then $X\in\mathrm{Pa}(s)\cap\mathrm{seen}(X)=K$: the predictor is one of the kept parents. The soundness statement of [8.3](#83-justifying-the-deletion-one-set-two-certificates-qmdagfritz_certificate) asks that in every classical model with $s=g(X)$, $s$ be a function of $K$ given the hypotheses. But $s=g(X)$ *is* a function of $K$, since $X\in K$. There is nothing to test: the d-separation condition $\mathbf X\setminus K\perp_d D\mid K$ has an empty left-hand side, and [`fritz_certificate`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L683) returns `dsep` without calling networkx. This is why a parent predictor is never a case for the LP: the LP is consulted only when the d-separation argument fails, and here it cannot fail. (The LP's `relabel` target set would not even apply, since $K$ minus the noise is not a single facet.)

What a parent predictor *does* for $s$ is modest. It deletes every parent of $s$ outside $\mathrm{seen}(X)$, but it must then leave: dropped, it is marginalized (it has the child $s$), which relays its own parents to $s$ and to its other children as edges and a classical facet; kept, it is split, the copy $X'$ predicts, and since $X'$ is a twin parent of $s$ the edge $X\to s$ itself lies in $D$ and is deleted, after which $X'$ is marginalized and relays $\mathrm{Pa}(X)$ to $s$ and a classical facet over $X$, $s$ and $X$'s other children. The third route of the visible-edge example in [6.5](#65-examples) shows the net effect: 1 loses 0→1 and Q{1,2} and gains C{0,1,3}. A parent predictor deletes what it cannot see and hands back a classical version of what it can, so its outputs are rarely closer to a seed than the input, and in the four-node census the `--parents` pool adds 254 structures and no proof ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)). The same happens to a predictor that is both a sibling and a parent of $s$: it is in the default pool through the facet, and its certificate is vacuous through the edge.

### 8.6 Joint target sets, and why not a second Fritz depth

For one predictor set and one pair of modes, the targets that d-separation certifies singly are known once the single steps are done. Every subset of two or more of them is then emitted as a **joint step**: the structure carrying all the splits is built (in copy mode every member is split), each member's deletion is read off again on that structure and its d-separation test is re-run there, every member is restricted at once, and the predictors leave as usual. Theorem [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) is stated for a set of targets, and its pull-back replaces the kernels one at a time, so a joint step is as sound as its members. Only d-separation members are admitted: the LP's `relabel` certificate substitutes the target for one of its latents, and two such substitutions for two targets do not obviously compose into a model of the joint candidate (a joint LP over the joint candidate with the `markov` target set would, and is an open option, Section [10](#10-open-questions)).

Why joint steps rather than a deeper search: with the predictor *dropped*, the joint step of the triangle ([6.5](#65-examples)) has no sequential counterpart at all, since after one copy is predicted the predictor is gone; the sequential route needs two *kept*-predictor steps and a point distribution. The cascade of [9.2](#92-two-phases-eight-fritz-stages) applies the Fritz trick once per input ("depth one"), so without joint steps the search would miss the paradigmatic derivation of Bell from the triangle, and would in general miss every output that needs a predictor to serve two targets. A second Fritz depth would recover them at the cost of re-expanding every Fritz output under the trick, which an earlier census measured at far more than everything else combined; the joint steps recover them for one extra surgery per subset of already-certified targets and no LP at all. In four nodes a predictor has at most three certified targets, so at most four joint subsets are added per predictor and modes. Joint steps belong to the d-separation stages of the cascade only ([8.8](#88-two-kinds-of-stage-d-separation-breadth-first-then-the-lp-with-early-exit)); `max_targets` bounds the subsets (all by default; 1 switches joint steps off), and the census shows them to be cheap: the four d-separation stages take five seconds in all, joint steps included, and they prove no four-node input that single steps did not, as the earlier joint-target code did not either.

### 8.7 Modes and the surgery (`QmDAG._restrict_targets`, `QmDAG.fritz_realise`)

The predicted-node mode and the predictor mode of [6.2](#62-node-splitting-and-the-two-predictor-modes-qmdagsplit_node) are applied before the deletion is read off, so that [8.2](#82-what-a-predictor-sees-and-the-deletion-it-dictates-qmdagfritz_deletion) and [8.3](#83-justifying-the-deletion-one-set-two-certificates-qmdagfritz_certificate) run on the structure that carries the splits: in `copy` mode each target is split first and the copies are the targets; in `kept` mode each childful predictor is split and the copy predicts. The surgery itself has no certificate logic. Each target keeps exactly its $K$: a kept facet stays quantum for its other members and becomes classical for $s$; every other node is untouched. Then the predictors leave: dropped predictors are deleted when childless and marginalized with teleportation otherwise; kept predictors leave their copies to the same fate, a childless kept predictor staying untouched. When several nodes are marginalized every order is realised and recorded, since teleportation is order-dependent (Section [3](#3-marginalization-naive-and-with-teleportation-naive_marginalization-teleportation_marginalization)). Outputs carry plain integer labels; a copy made by splitting is the next free index and is printed with a prime in the recorded parameters. Each recorded step states the targets, the mode, the deleted parents of each target (visible parents by index, facets as `Q{…}` or `C{…}`, the noise omitted), the predictor set, the predictor mode, the certificate and, when several nodes were marginalized, the order.

### 8.8 Two kinds of stage: d-separation breadth-first, then the LP with early exit

The cascade of [9.2](#92-two-phases-eight-fritz-stages) runs the Fritz trick in two groups of four stages, one per pair of modes each. In the **d-separation stages** [`fritz_steps`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L787) runs with the LP off: for every predictor set it visits every target, reads off the deletion, runs the d-separation test, emits the single steps and then the joint steps of that predictor, and the explorer records everything the root produces, breadth-first, with no early exit. The test is cheap, so there is nothing to save (the four d-separation stages take five seconds in the census, [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)), and recording every d-separation route keeps what is recorded, and with it the database of proven structures ([9.5](#95-the-known-gap-database-the-search-establishes)), independent of the order in which a root's predictors happen to be tried. (The joint steps themselves would survive early exit either way: [`fritz_steps`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L787) collects a predictor's certified targets before emitting any of its joint steps.) In the **LP stages** [`fritz_steps`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L787) runs with `lp_only`: the same loop, but a pair d-separation certifies is skipped (its step is already recorded), a pair it rejects goes to the LP, no joint sets are formed, and the explorer stops the root at the first child whose elementary reduction is a known gap, since here every candidate costs a solve.

The loop order is predictor-first in both groups. In the d-separation stages it has to be, for the joint sets; in the LP stages it is kept for consistency, and nothing is lost: the LP costs one solve per (predictor, target) pair whatever the order, and the per-structure part of the LP (Shannon cone and the Markov rows of the structure that carries the splits) is built once per structure and cached, so grouping the pairs by target, which would group them by split structure in copy mode, would only matter if that cache were smaller than the number of split structures of one input (at most a handful in four nodes, cache size 64).

### 8.9 What the procedure decides, and what it does not

Three choices of the procedure influence *which* certificate an input gets, and none influences *whether* it gets one.

* **Stage order** ([9.2](#92-two-phases-eight-fritz-stages)): every d-separation stage before every LP stage, and within each group replace mode before copy mode, dropped predictors before kept ones; each stage is applied only to the inputs the earlier stages left unproven. An input proven at stage $k$ never sees stage $k+1$, so an LP proof is never recorded for an input that any d-separation route settles. The kept-predictor example of [6.5](#65-examples) is the case in point: it has a d-separation route (copy mode, kept predictor) and an LP route (replace mode, kept predictor); the census records the former, where an earlier cascade that ran the LP within each pair of modes recorded the latter.
* **Early exit** ([8.8](#88-two-kinds-of-stage-d-separation-breadth-first-then-the-lp-with-early-exit)): within an LP stage, the first child whose elementary reduction reaches a known gap ends the input, so later alternatives of the same stage are not recorded; the d-separation stages record everything.
* **Shortest chain, earliest stage** ([`build_report`](../qc_gap_search.py#L524)): among the recorded routes, the certificate is the shortest chain of transitions using the transitions of the earliest stage that has one, ending at a seed or at an input already certified.

What is invariant under all three: the set of proven inputs, the cumulative count after each stage and the ladder of [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade). The ladder is computed from the recorded transitions by the rung each one belongs to, and an input proven at some rung has recorded the step that proves it there in every variant of the procedure (an unproven input records everything; a proven input is proven at the rung of the step that proved it). What varies: the certificate text, and the proven-structure database of [9.5](#95-the-known-gap-database-the-search-establishes), since alternative routes visit other intermediates (in four nodes 956 structures with or without the LP stages' early exit, since it never cuts a root short there, [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)). The variants were measured against each other on the census, and the counts agree ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)).

## 9. Search, certificates and the four-node census (`qc_gap_search.py`, `Special Applications/proving_QC_Gaps.py`)

### 9.1 The explorer and its certificates

[`ClosureExplorer`](../qc_gap_search.py#L174) expands every reachable structure, up to relabelling, exactly once under a set of tricks and records each [`Transition(trick, params, source, target)`](../qc_gap_search.py#L39). Every transformation returns a plainly indexed structure; a copy made by node splitting is the next free index, and the recorded parameters print it with a prime (0′ is the copy of 0). Each unlabelled id has one stored representative, every trick is applied to that representative, and the recorded params are stated in its labels; certificates print the representative above each step. Keying on unlabelled ids is legitimate because every piggyback is label-equivariant. The order dependence of Section [3](#3-marginalization-naive-and-with-teleportation-naive_marginalization-teleportation_marginalization) is enumerated rather than fixed because different removal orders give different structures and none is canonical.

### 9.2 Two phases, eight Fritz stages

The census runs in two phases over one shared explorer. Throughout, $s$ is the predicted node and $X$ the predictor of a Fritz step.

*Phase 1 (cheap).* Only the elementary reductions act, closed over everything reachable from every input, and only the three-node seeds are known, so the Bell variants are inputs like any other. Reachability is transitive within this phase, so the set of inputs it proves does not depend on the order in which the tricks are applied, and [`_fixpoint`](../qc_gap_search.py#L443) iterates the implication closure among the inputs until nothing changes. Because every elementary trick is cheap and the phase is exhaustive, it is the one phase where "what does this trick prove alone, and what is lost without it" is a meaningful and affordable question, and [1.1](#11-phase-1-results-the-elementary-piggybacks-from-the-three-node-seeds) reports exactly that, per elementary trick.

*Phase 2 (expensive).* The weakest Bell variants join the seeds (every Bell variant has a gap by the direct argument, and those not reachable from the three-node seeds, such as [`QG_Bell_C_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L78), can only be seeds), the gaps of phase 1 and of the cache ([9.4](#94-the-cache)) are known, and the inputs the cheap phase left unproven are attacked by a cascade of **eight Fritz stages of increasing cost** ([`default_stages`](../qc_gap_search.py#L111), [`CASCADE`](../qc_gap_search.py#L641)), each applied once to each input the previous stages left unproven. The first four certify by d-separation, in the order

1. replace mode, dropped predictors;
2. replace mode, kept predictors (childless untouched, childful split and the copy marginalized, [6.2](#62-node-splitting-and-the-two-predictor-modes-qmdagsplit_node));
3. copy mode (the predicted node is split, [6.2](#62-node-splitting-and-the-two-predictor-modes-qmdagsplit_node)), dropped predictors;
4. copy mode, kept predictors;

and the last four repeat the same modes with the entropic LP of Section [7](#7-the-entropic-certificate-for-the-fritz-piggyback-qmdag_entropic_certificate) (`lp_only`: the pairs d-separation certified are already recorded, only the pairs it rejected go to the LP). Every d-separation stage precedes every LP stage because the LP is the only expensive thing in the search, and within each group replace mode precedes copy mode because copy mode splits the target and so works on a larger structure, dropped predictors precede kept ones because a kept predictor leaves the node count unchanged and, when childful, adds a split and a marginalization. Each stage applies the trick once to each input still unproven, and the outputs are then reduced with the elementary tricks only, never with another Fritz step ("depth one"). The explorer records the stage in which each transition was found, so the cumulative count of inputs proven after each stage is a graph query ([`GapReport.stage_counts`](../qc_gap_search.py#L403)); the ladder of [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade) ([`ladder`](../qc_gap_search.py#L676)) computes the same nine counts from the recorded parameters (certificate, mode, predictor mode) instead of the recorded stage, and the slow test checks that the two agree.

*Breadth-first, then early exit* ([8.8](#88-two-kinds-of-stage-d-separation-breadth-first-then-the-lp-with-early-exit)). In the d-separation stages each input is recorded in full: the test is cheap, and recording every route keeps the record independent of the order in which predictors are tried. In the LP stages each input is handled lazily ([`ClosureExplorer.extend`](../qc_gap_search.py#L276) with `stop_when_known`): each emitted child is recorded and reduced with the elementary tricks at once, and the input stops as soon as a child or its reduction is a seed, a cached gap or an input proven in an earlier stage. Every recorded transition is a sound step, so the partial record is sound; the cumulative counts and the ladder do not depend on where an input stops, since an unproven input records everything and a proven input is proven at the stage of the step that proved it either way. In the four-node census it saves nothing measurable: the same 714 LP solves are run with and without it ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)), because for each of the five inputs the LP proves, the certifying candidate is the last one its LP stage tries. Early exit is there for larger inputs, where an LP stage has more candidates per root than four-node structures offer.

The cascade loses proofs that would need two Fritz steps, which is accepted: a Fritz output has at least as many visible nodes as its source, and re-expanding every such output under the Fritz trick was found to be far slower than everything else combined. The counts of [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade) are therefore lower bounds for what the tricks can prove, exact for phase 1. What the order decides is cost (the LP runs only where the cheap stages failed) and which tricks a certificate prefers ([`build_report`](../qc_gap_search.py#L524) takes the shortest chain within the earliest stage that has one). [`prove_gaps`](../qc_gap_search.py#L465) closes the proven set under implication from the seeds and attaches to every proven input a certificate, the shortest chain of transitions down to a named seed.

### 9.3 What is never computed

The search never asks how many inputs an expensive piggyback proves *alone*: that would mean closing the search under that piggyback over everything reachable, which is exactly the cost the cascade avoids, and it would answer a question nobody needs answered. The expensive steps are assessed by the cumulative ladder of [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade) alone: what each successive stage adds to everything cheaper.

### 9.4 The cache

([`gap_cache.py`](../gap_cache.py), [`cache/known_gaps.json`](../cache/known_gaps.json).) Every proven input is stored with its unlabelled id, its structure, the seed its certificate ends at, the chain of transitions (trick, parameters, source and target ids), the rendered certificate, and the **version** of every piggyback the chain uses ([`PIGGYBACK_VERSIONS`](../qc_gap_search.py#L23) in [`qc_gap_search.py`](../qc_gap_search.py)). A certificate that ends at a cached gap inherits that entry's versions, so dependence is transitive. When a piggyback is corrected its version is bumped, and loading the cache drops exactly the entries whose proof relied on it; everything else stays known, and the expensive stages run only on the inputs that are neither cached nor proven by phase 1. The entries are up to relabelling, like everything else.

### 9.5 The known-gap database the search establishes

Every structure the search touches, inputs, intermediates and the hybrid structures with classical facets that the Fritz steps create, is a proven QC gap as soon as it reaches a seed, and [`GapReport.proven_structure_ids`](../qc_gap_search.py#L421) returns that set. This is the database to keep: the census inputs have every latent quantum, but a structure with some facets classical is a *weaker* structure, its gap is not implied by the gap of the all-quantum version (making a facet classical shrinks the quantum set and leaves the classical set), and it is a gap only if a chain of piggybacks from it reaches a seed. Fritz steps are indifferent to whether the shared facet is classical or quantum, so chains often transfer; conditioning and teleportation are not, since they create quantum facets only among quantum siblings. [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade) reports how many hybrid structures the census proves in passing.

**The database is not the set of four-node structures the cheap tricks can prove.** The inputs are the all-quantum structures, so a hybrid structure enters the database only when some chain from an all-quantum input passes through it. The intermediate G(3) of the kept-predictor example of [6.5](#65-examples), 0→1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, C{0,1,3′}, C{0,1,2}, is proven by one teleportation marginalization to [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41); it was in the database of the earlier census because the recorded chain for that input passed through it, and it is absent from the current one because the cascade now records the shorter LP route for that input. A hybrid structure that no recorded chain happens to visit is absent even when a single elementary step proves it. The size of the omission: recolouring each facet of each all-quantum input classical or quantum gives 8756 distinct four-node structures with at least one quantum facet, and the elementary closure over all of them from the seeds (21 seconds) proves 5073, of which 4143 are not in the census database. G(3) is not even among these recolourings: its quantum facets Q{0,1} and Q{0,2} lie strictly inside its classical facets, a shape that only arises when a facet loses a quantum member, so the hybrid input space is larger still. Hybrid structures as *inputs* are therefore a cheap and worthwhile extension of the census (Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks)), and the right place to apply the elementary tricks exhaustively; with the degradation piggyback of [0.4](#04-the-degradation-piggyback-quantum-source-to-classical-source) as a trick, only the weakest colouring of each proven structure would need storing.

### 9.6 Reading the tables, and why the seeds matter

Reachability restricted to any subset of tricks, or to any predicate on the recorded parameters, is a graph query over the recorded transitions. In [1.1](#11-phase-1-results-the-elementary-piggybacks-from-the-three-node-seeds), "via" counts the inputs provable with one elementary trick alone (the degradation lookup always allowed) and "only via" the inputs lost when that trick is removed and everything else kept; both are computed in phase 1 with the three-node seeds. In [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade) the counts are cumulative over the rungs. Since each stage of the cascade ran only on inputs the earlier stages left unproven, an input counted at an early rung is one the cheaper combination of modes and certificate proves, not one the later ones could not prove: kept predictors subsume dropped ones and the LP subsumes d-separation (Sections [6.2](#62-node-splitting-and-the-two-predictor-modes-qmdagsplit_node), [7.4](#74-why-relabel-is-justified)).

Every piggyback except Fritz reduces the number of visible nodes or leaves the structure's nodes alone (degradation), so for four-node inputs a four-node seed can only be reached by a Fritz step or a degradation. Listing the Bell variants as seeds in phase 2 is what lets the Fritz steps conclude in one move; keeping them out of phase 1 is what lets the reductions get credit for the Bell variants they do reach ([1.1](#11-phase-1-results-the-elementary-piggybacks-from-the-three-node-seeds)). The Bell variants that are not derivable from the three-node seeds by any piggyback have to be seeds, on the strength of the direct Bell argument.

### 9.7 Entropic certificates attempted

Per candidate single-target step (predictor set, target, the deletion read off) over the eight stages, split structures included and the re-checks of joint target sets not counted ([`ENTROPIC_STATS`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L57)): certified by d-separation 802, of which 404 vacuously (the predictor a parent of the target, [8.5](#85-parent-predictors-why-the-certificate-is-vacuous)) and 398 by the test proper; beyond d-separation, `relabel` 54, failed 668. The `markov` target set is off ([7.3](#73-the-certificate-and-its-two-target-sets-qmdag_entropic_certificate)); in an earlier census that had it on, together with joint LP targets and extra deletions (Section [10](#10-open-questions)), `markov` certified 91 candidates after `relabel` had failed, the joint targets 27 and the extra deletions hundreds, and none of these decided an input. No solve hit the time limit. Among candidates that d-separation rejects, the LP certifies roughly one in thirteen. Success is far from universal, and a failure of the LP is not a proof that the implication is false ([7.4](#74-why-relabel-is-justified)); [8.3](#83-justifying-the-deletion-one-set-two-certificates-qmdagfritz_certificate) shows a failure that is a true negative. Almost none of these certified steps is decisive ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)): their outputs are structures the cheaper tricks reach as well, or structures that are not gaps as far as the seeds know. The counts are the same with and without the LP stages' early exit ([1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade)).

### 9.8 Why node stitching and naive marginalization show few exclusive proofs

**Node stitching.** Its characteristic product is a Bell variant derived from an instrumental variant. In phase 1 it is the sole route for exactly the three Bell variants that are proven at all ([1.1](#11-phase-1-results-the-elementary-piggybacks-from-the-three-node-seeds)); every other input it proves (ten in all) is also reachable by PD or conditioning. Node stitching is the only reduction that re-uses an outcome as a setting; how much it contributes is bounded by how many Bell-type structures the input set contains that the seed list does not already hold.

**The two marginalizations.** They coincide at a node without a quantum facet, so naive marginalization alone is never exclusive: wherever it applies, the teleportation version makes the same move. The 16 inputs lost without teleportation marginalization are where the removed node holds a quantum share and relaying it to the node's children is what the proof needs. Conditioning covers part of the same ground, since conditioning on a node $v$ without visible parents adds a quantum facet over all quantum siblings of $v$ (entanglement swapping), the same connection teleportation relays to the children of $v$, only not restricted to children; what conditioning cannot provide is the relayed visible input of $v$, and where that input matters, or where conditioning is blocked by a grandparent, teleportation marginalization is the route. The earlier census, which closed the dropped-predictor Fritz trick over everything reachable and had the Bell variants as seeds, found alternative routes for all of these; the cheap phase, with the elementary tricks alone and the three-node seeds, does not.

**Example** (only via teleportation marginalization). Input: 0→1→2; Q{0,1,3}, Q{0,2}. Node 0 is the setting of 1, holds a share of a tripartite state with 1 and 3, and a bipartite state with 2. Marginalizing 0 with teleportation relays its share of Q{0,1,3} and of Q{0,2} to its child 1: 1→2; Q{1,3}, Q{1,2}, whose degradation C{1,3}, Q{1,2} is [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41). Naive marginalization of 0 relays only a classical common cause to 1 and 2 and leaves 1→2; C{1,2}, Q{1,3}, not a known gap; conditioning on 0 gives 1→2; Q{1,2,3}, PD on 0 gives 1→2; Q{1,3}, neither a known gap; node stitching needs an exogenous node, and 0 holds facets; and no reduction at 1, 2 or 3 reaches a seed either, since each either destroys the setting or isolates 3. A second example of the same shape, 0→1, 1→2, 1→3 with Q{0,1,2}, Q{0,3}, relays the shares of 0 to 1 and gives [`QG_Evans`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L43).

**Example** (only via marginalization, either kind). Input: 2→3; Q{0,1}, Q{0,3}, Q{1,2}. Node 2 holds one share, of Q{1,2}, and feeds 3. Marginalizing 2 relays that share to 3 as a classical facet: Q{0,1}, Q{0,3}, C{1,3}, which degrades (Q{0,3} made classical) to [`QG_Triangle`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L42). Teleportation has nothing to add here, because 3 shares no quantum facet with 2 and so there is no entangled pair to teleport through (Section [3](#3-marginalization-naive-and-with-teleportation-naive_marginalization-teleportation_marginalization)); both marginalizations give the same structure, and the input counts in the "either kind" row but in neither single row. PD on 2 or conditioning on 2 leaves Q{0,1}, Q{0,3} without the third side of the triangle.

## 10. Open questions

* **Completeness of the entropic certificate.** The failures recorded in [`ENTROPIC_STATS`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L57) are failures of the LP, not necessarily of the piggyback ([7.4](#74-why-relabel-is-justified)). Substituting other sets of variables for the latents of $G'$, beyond $\lbrace L\rbrace$ and $\lbrace s\rbrace$, is the natural next level: each substitution is expressible because the LP indexes joint entropies of sets, and the soundness proof is that of [7.4](#74-why-relabel-is-justified).
* **Sub-maximal deletions.** The deletion of a Fritz step is all of $\mathrm{Pa}(s)\setminus\mathrm{seen}(\mathbf X)$ or nothing (Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks)): a predictor that cannot see a kept parent cannot announce $f(K)$. There is a sound way around it. Let $U=K\setminus\mathrm{seen}(\mathbf X)$ be the kept parents the predictors cannot see. In the lift, $s$ computes $f(\mathrm{common}(s),U)$ and each predictor announces the *table* $u\mapsto f(\mathrm{common}(s),u)$, a subvariable from which $s$ is recovered given $U$; the hypothesis becomes $H(s\mid\mathbf X,U)=0$, and the pull-back has to show that a classical model of $G$ satisfying it is a model of the candidate in which $s$ keeps $K$. Neither Theorem [6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate) nor Theorem [7.5](#75-theorem-entropic-certificate) covers this hypothesis as stated, so it is a new theorem, not a flag. The lead's observation that in four nodes the maximal deletion minus the noise almost always has a single element says that it would touch few targets; it may matter for larger structures, where $s$ has more parents than a single predictor can see.
* **Extra deletions certified by the same LP.** With $s$ the predicted node and $\mathbf X$ the predicting set, the hypotheses of [7.3](#73-the-certificate-and-its-two-target-sets-qmdag_entropic_certificate) do not mention which node a deletion concerns, so the same LP can certify deleting a parent $p$ of any non-predictor node $t$ through the per-edge target $I(t{:}p\mid\mathrm{Pa}'(t)\setminus p)\le0$, where $\mathrm{Pa}'(t)$ are the parents of $t$ in the current candidate; deleting an edge enlarges $\mathcal I(G_1)$, so the hypotheses would be recomputed after each deletion and the loop run to a fixed point, and the final candidate verified as a whole with [7.3](#73-the-certificate-and-its-two-target-sets-qmdag_entropic_certificate), whose soundness proof (Theorem [7.5](#75-theorem-entropic-certificate)) already allows further parents deleted from non-predictor nodes. An earlier version of the code had this loop; in a census run with it on, extra deletions certified hundreds of candidate steps and decided no input, so it was removed rather than kept as a dead option. Two constraints any reimplementation must respect: deletions never touch a predictor (it must keep seeing $\mathrm{common}(s)$), and never remove the last channel from the predictors to the predicted node, since without it the hypotheses $H(s\mid\mathbf X)=0$ and $s\perp\mathbf X$ (an observable d-separation of the resulting $G_1$) are jointly satisfiable only by a constant $s$, the LP would certify a vacuous statement, and the lift would no longer exist.
* **Joint target sets certified by the LP.** Joint steps are admitted on d-separation certificates only ([8.6](#86-joint-target-sets-and-why-not-a-second-fritz-depth)). A joint LP, one candidate restricting every target of the set, one hypothesis $H(s_i\mid\mathbf X)=0$ per target and the `markov` target set, is sound by Theorem [7.5](#75-theorem-entropic-certificate) read for a set of targets; an earlier version of the code ran it and it never decided a four-node input. Whether the `relabel` certificates of two targets can be composed, so that a set with LP-certified members could be admitted without the joint LP, is open.
* **The prediction-free edge-deletion piggyback** (KPC, Corollary 3): the same LP without the perfect-prediction hypothesis certifies deleting edges justified by the new graph's independences alone. The code can check it ([`QmDAG._entropic_certificate(..., predicted=())`](../quantum_mDAG.py#L517)), but it is not exposed as a trick.
* **Hybrid inputs.** The census inputs have every facet quantum, and [9.5](#95-the-known-gap-database-the-search-establishes) shows that this leaves out thousands of four-node structures with classical facets that the elementary tricks prove in seconds, among them structures whose quantum facets lie inside classical ones, which no recolouring of an all-quantum input produces. Enumerating all pairs (classical complex, quantum complex) on four nodes and running the cheap phase on them is the direct fix; running the piggybacks in reverse from the seeds is the other ([Appendix A](#appendix-a-the-piggybacks-in-reverse)).
* **Exact certificates.** Farkas multipliers are floating point; rationalising them and re-verifying the combination exactly is cheap and would make every entropic step a checkable proof.
* **The remaining structures.** The 63 unproven inputs are listed below. Every one of them contains a visible edge out of a node that also holds quantum facets, and most contain a chain of two or three visible edges (the labellings are the representatives the search stored, not necessarily the temporally ordered ones).

## Appendix A. The piggybacks in reverse

### A.0 Why the search runs forward

Every piggyback is an implication, "a QC gap in $G'$ implies a QC gap in $G$", and the search of Section [9](#9-search-certificates-and-the-four-node-census-qc_gap_searchpy-special-applicationsproving_qc_gapspy) applies it forward: it starts from a candidate $G$, maps it down through piggybacks until a known gap is reached, and so proves exactly the candidates it is given. Run in reverse, the same implications generate gaps: for a known gap $G'$, every structure in the inverse image of a piggyback is a gap, and the closure of the seeds under all inverse images is the set of every structure the toolkit can prove. The two directions answer different questions.

The question this work asks is: *which causal structures with quantum latents admit correlations that no classical model of the same structure admits?* Its natural input set is the list of all-quantum structures on $n$ visible nodes, and the forward search answers it input by input, with a certificate for each. In that setting the reverse closure buys nothing: the all-quantum four-node structures are a finite list of 996, each is tested directly, and a reverse closure restricted to four all-quantum nodes would produce the same 931 structures by a longer route.

The other question, *which hybrid structures, with some sources classical and some quantum, admit a QC gap?*, has a much larger input set ([9.5](#95-the-known-gap-database-the-search-establishes): already 8756 recolourings of the all-quantum inputs, and more structures whose quantum facets lie inside classical ones), most of it never a candidate anyone asks about. There the reverse direction is the efficient one: the inverse images of the seeds produce exactly the hybrid structures that are gaps, classical facets arising naturally where a reversed marginalization or degradation puts them, without listing the inputs first. The code is designed for the first question; this appendix records how each piggyback inverts, so that the second can be attacked when wanted. Throughout, $G'$ is the known gap and $G$ a structure in the inverse image, so that $G\mapsto G'$ is the forward map of the section cited. Inverse images are finite once the number of visible nodes of $G$ is bounded, and every member is a gap by the forward theorem, with no new proof needed.

### A.1 Degradation in reverse: quantumification

Forward ([0.4](#04-the-degradation-piggyback-quantum-source-to-classical-source)): make some quantum facets of $G$ classical. Inverse: upgrade any set of classical facets of $G'$ to quantum, or add a quantum facet inside an existing classical facet (which is the same as upgrading part of it). The inverse image of $G'$ with $c$ classical facets of sizes $m_1,\dots,m_c$ has at most $\prod_i 2^{2^{m_i}}$ members, in practice a few dozen for four nodes. Example ([0.4](#04-the-degradation-piggyback-quantum-source-to-classical-source)): from G(3) = 0→1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, C{0,1,3}, C{0,1,2}, all of 0→1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, Q{1,2}, C{0,1,3}, C{0,1,2}; 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{1,2}, Q{0,1,3}, C{0,1,2}; 0→1→2; C{0,1,3}, Q{0,1,2}; up to 0→1→2; Q{0,1,3}, Q{0,1,2}. Every member of the inverse image is a gap. This is the one inverse that never changes a node or an edge, and the reason the seeds are stored in weakest form.

### A.2 Point distribution in reverse: node insertion

Forward (Section [2](#2-point-distribution-pd)): remove a node $x$ with its edges and its facet memberships. Inverse: insert a node $x$ into $G'$ with any set of visible parents, any set of visible children not creating a cycle, and membership in any facets, new or existing, classical or quantum. The inverse image is large (every placement of one node) but finite, and every member is a gap because fixing $x$ to a point distribution gives $G'$ back. Strictly hybrid example: insert into [`QG_Instrumental_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L39) = 0→1→2; Q{1,2} a node 3 that shares a classical facet with the setting, 0→1→2; Q{1,2}, C{0,3}: a gap, although it is neither all quantum nor a recolouring of an all-quantum structure in which C{0,3} is the only facet on 0 and 3 (its all-quantum version Q{0,3} is a census input, proven by PD on 3 as well). The reverse search would list it from the seed; the forward census never sees it.

### A.3 Marginalization in reverse: node insertion along edges and facets

Forward (Section [3](#3-marginalization-naive-and-with-teleportation-naive_marginalization-teleportation_marginalization)): remove $v$, relay its visible parents to its children, turn each facet $F\ni v$ into $F\setminus v$ plus a classical facet over $(F\setminus v)$ and the children of $v$, add a classical facet over the children (the private randomness of $v$), and, with teleportation, extend each quantum facet $F\ni v$ to $(F\setminus v)\cup T$ for the children $T$ that share a quantum facet with $v$. Inverse: choose in $G'$ a set of nodes to become the children of $v$, a set of their parents to become the parents of $v$ instead (the edges $p\to c$ are then explained as $p\to v\to c$ and may be removed), and for each classical facet $C$ of $G'$ that contains the children an explanation as the relay of a facet $F\ni v$ with $F\setminus v=C\setminus T$; a quantum facet of $G'$ over $(F\setminus v)\cup T$ may be explained as the teleportation of a facet $F\ni v$, which requires $v$ to share a quantum facet with each member of $T$. Each explanation is one member of the inverse image.

Strictly hybrid examples. (i) The reverse of the first step of the Section [3](#3-marginalization-naive-and-with-teleportation-naive_marginalization-teleportation_marginalization) example: [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41) = 1→2; C{0,1}, Q{1,2} is the naive marginalization of $v$ in $v$→1→2; C{0,$v$}, Q{1,2}: the classical facet C{0,1} of the seed is the relay of C{0,$v$} to $v$'s child 1. (ii) The reverse of the last step of the kept-predictor example of [6.5](#65-examples): [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41) written as 1→2; Q{1,2}, C{1,3′} is the teleportation marginalization of 0 in G(3) = 0→1→2; Q{0,1}, Q{0,2}, C{0,1,3′}, C{0,1,2}: the quantum facet Q{1,2} of the seed is explained as Q{0,2} teleported to 1 through Q{0,1}, and the classical facets as relays of C{0,1,3′} and of 0's private randomness. G(3) has classical facets that strictly contain quantum ones, a shape no recolouring of an all-quantum input has; the forward census met it only as an intermediate of a chain that the current cascade no longer records ([9.5](#95-the-known-gap-database-the-search-establishes)), while the reverse search finds it directly from the seed.

### A.4 Conditioning in reverse: un-swapping entanglement

Forward (Section [4](#4-conditioning-conditioning)): remove $x$, whose facets all lie within its latent siblings $S$ plus $x$ (condition 2); every facet containing $x$ loses $x$; the quantum siblings of $x$ gain a joint quantum facet; the visible children of $x$ lose their edge from $x$; the visible parents of $x$ are handled by conditions 1 and 3. Inverse: choose in $G'$ a quantum facet $Q\lbrace S_q\rbrace$ to be explained as the swap, insert $x$ with quantum facets $F_i\ni x$ such that $\bigcup_i (F_i\setminus x)=S_q$ (each $F_i\setminus x$ then also appears in $G'$, possibly absorbed into $Q\lbrace S_q\rbrace$), optionally classical facets $F\ni x$ whose $F\setminus x$ appear in $G'$, optionally edges from $x$ to nodes of $G'$, and visible parents of $x$ only as conditions 1 and 3 allow. Strictly hybrid example: [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41) = 1→2; C{0,1}, Q{1,2} is the conditioning on $x$ of 1→2; C{0,1}, Q{1,$x$}, Q{$x$,2}: the quantum link between treatment and outcome is explained as entanglement swapped through $x$, while the classical facet with the instrument stays as it is. The reverse of the conditioning step in the kept-predictor example of [6.5](#65-examples), G(2) → G(3), is another: G(2) = 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{0,3}, Q{1,3}, C{0,1,3,3′}, C{0,1,2} explains the Q{0,1} of G(3) as a swap through 3 and the C{0,1,3′} as the relic of C{0,1,3,3′}.

### A.5 Node stitching in reverse: interruption

Forward (Section [5](#5-node-stitching-node_stitching-formerly-interruption)): remove the exogenous node $x$ and give its visible children to the sink $y$. Inverse, which is the operation that deserves the name *interruption*: choose a node $y$ of $G'$ and a subset of its visible children, cut those children off from $y$ and give them to a new exogenous node $x$ (no visible parents, no facets), with $y$ not a descendant of $x$, which is automatic. The inverse image is one member per node and subset of its children, so it is tiny and exact. Examples: [`QG_Instrumental_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L39) = 0→1→2; Q{1,2}, interrupted at $y=1$ with the child 2 given to $x$, is 0→1, $x$→2; Q{1,2}, which is [`QG_Bell_Edge_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L83); the same interruption of [`QG_Instrumental_EdgeC`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L40) = 0→1→2; C{0,1}, Q{1,2} gives 0→1, $x$→2; C{0,1}, Q{1,2}, which is [`QG_Bell_EdgeC_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L82); and of [`QG_Instrumental_C`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L41) = 1→2; C{0,1}, Q{1,2} gives $x$→2; C{0,1}, Q{1,2}, which is [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80), a strictly hybrid structure. The three Bell variants that phase 1 proves ([1.1](#11-phase-1-results-the-elementary-piggybacks-from-the-three-node-seeds)) are exactly the interruptions of the three instrumental seeds, found forward because they happen to be inputs; the reverse search would find them, and the hybrid ones, directly.

### A.6 The Fritz piggyback in reverse

Forward (Section [6](#6-the-fritz-piggyback-fritz)): for a predictor set $\mathbf X$ and a predicted node $s$, restrict $s$ to $\mathrm{common}(s)$, whose facets become classical for $s$, and remove the predictors (dropped) or keep them (childless untouched, childful split and the copy marginalized). Inverse, replace mode: choose in $G'$ a node $s$ all of whose latent parents are classical facets (the former $\mathrm{common}(s)$, which in $G$ may be quantum again, with the classical copy removed), choose the predictor set, either present in $G'$ (kept) or to be inserted (dropped: a new node $X$ that shares with $s$ at least one of those facets and may have any other parents and facets, the facets of $X$ that were relayed to its children by the marginalization being un-relayed as in [A.3](#a3-marginalization-in-reverse-node-insertion-along-edges-and-facets)), and choose a set $D$ of further parents to give $s$: visible parents or facets, any that the predictor cannot see, and then *all* of those, since the forward deletion is maximal (Section [8](#8-the-fritz-search-procedure-qmdagfritz_steps-qc_gap_searchfritz_tricks)): every parent of $s$ in the reconstructed $G$ that the predictor cannot see must lie in $D$. Then check the certificate on the reconstructed $G$, by d-separation ([6.3](#63-theorem-d-separation-certificate)) or by the LP ([7.5](#75-theorem-entropic-certificate)): unlike the inverses above, this one does not come for free, because the certificate is a property of $G$ as a whole, of how $\mathbf X$, $\mathrm{common}(s)$ and $D$ relate. The members of the inverse image are the reconstructions that pass. Example ([7.8](#78-further-examples-from-the-census) reversed): [`QG_Bell_C_Edge`](../known_QC_gaps.py#L80) written as 0→1; C{2,3}, Q{1,3} has $s=2$ with the single classical facet C{2,3} and the childless predictor $X=3$ present; giving 2 the parents 1→2 and Q{0,2} and making C{2,3} quantum again reconstructs 0→1→2; Q{0,2}, Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}, and the LP certifies it; giving 2 the parents 0→2 and 1→2 instead reconstructs 0→1, 0→2, 1→2; Q{1,3}, Q{2,3}, also certified. Both are census inputs proven forward, and both are found from the seed in reverse.

Copy mode inverts in two moves. The forward step splits $s$ into $s$ and $s'$ and restricts $s'$, so $G'$ has one more visible node than the structure whose gap is at stake, and the inverse must first reconstruct a structure $G_+$ with $n+1$ visible nodes in which $s'$ has its restricted parents and $s$ its full ones, then merge $s$ and $s'$ ([A.7](#a7-node-splitting-in-reverse-merging)). The reverse search for four-node gaps therefore passes through five-node structures, exactly as the forward search does.

### A.7 Node splitting in reverse: merging

Forward ([6.2](#62-node-splitting-and-the-two-predictor-modes-qmdagsplit_node)): replace $s$ by two nodes with the same parents, the same children and a shared classical pair facet. Inverse: merge two nodes of $G'$ that have the same parents and the same children into one (their shared pair facet, if explicit, disappears). Node splitting is never run on its own forward and would not be in reverse either; merging is the second move of the reversed copy mode ([A.6](#a6-the-fritz-piggyback-in-reverse)) and nothing else.

### A.8 What a reverse closure would compute

Starting from the weakest seeds and closing under [A.1](#a1-degradation-in-reverse-quantumification) to [A.7](#a7-node-splitting-in-reverse-merging) with the number of visible nodes bounded by $n$ (and $n+1$ for the intermediate of [A.6](#a6-the-fritz-piggyback-in-reverse)) gives every structure on at most $n$ nodes that the toolkit can prove, hybrid structures included, each with a certificate read off the inverse steps. The inverse images of [A.1](#a1-degradation-in-reverse-quantumification), [A.2](#a2-point-distribution-in-reverse-node-insertion) and [A.3](#a3-marginalization-in-reverse-node-insertion-along-edges-and-facets) are the large ones; [A.4](#a4-conditioning-in-reverse-un-swapping-entanglement) to [A.7](#a7-node-splitting-in-reverse-merging) are small. Because the degradation inverse ([A.1](#a1-degradation-in-reverse-quantumification)) is cheap and closes upward, the closure can again be stored in weakest form only. Two things the reverse closure does not change: the set of all-quantum four-node gaps the toolkit proves, which is the 931 of [1.2](#12-phase-2-results-the-cascade) by construction, and the 63 inputs it leaves open, since an input is proven forward iff it lies in the reverse closure.
